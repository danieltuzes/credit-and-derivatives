## What a CDS is

A credit default swap (CDS) is a bilateral derivative on a named reference
entity. The protection buyer pays a running premium to the protection seller.
If a covered credit event occurs before maturity, the seller makes a
loss-related protection payment. Unlike buying the entity's bond, entering a
CDS does not itself lend principal to the reference entity. Tuckman and Serrat
describe the fee and contingent legs in Chapter 14[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.5, printed pp. 361–362]; Hull describes the same buyer/seller cash-flow directions
and quarterly premium convention[@hull-options-futures; §24.1, printed pp. 548–550].

This lesson reports both leg present values as positive magnitudes. From the
protection buyer's perspective,

$$
\explain{cds-protection-buyer-net-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{buyer}}}
=\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
-\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}.
$$

The seller has the opposite signed value in this two-party model.

## Start from disjoint events

Let the random [[cds-default-time]] be $\tau$. Use the local
[[cds-period-index]] $i$ across the
[[number-of-cds-periods]] $n$. The schedule has dates
$0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=T$. Define the disjoint interval events

$$
\explain{cds-default-event}{G_i}
=\{\explain{payment-time}{t_{i-1}}<
\explain{cds-default-time}{\tau}\leq
\explain{payment-time}{t_i}\},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
$$

and add $\explain{cds-default-event}{G_0}=\{\tau>T\}$ for survival through
maturity. These events are
disjoint and exhaustive under the one-default assumption.

For a local [[cds-event-payoff]] $Y$ that is constant at $y_i$ on each
event,

$$
\explain{risk-neutral-expectation}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[Y]}
=\sum_{i=0}^{n}
\explain{cds-event-payoff-value}{y_i}\,
\explain{risk-neutral-probability-measure}{\mathbb{Q}}
(\explain{cds-default-event}{G_i}).
$$

This is the finite expectation rule from the probability lesson, now using
risk-neutral pricing weights. If $Y$ is not constant within an interval, the
correct identity is

$$
\explain{risk-neutral-expectation}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[Y]}
=\sum_{i=0}^{n}
\explain{risk-neutral-probability-measure}{\mathbb{Q}}
(\explain{cds-default-event}{G_i})
\explain{risk-neutral-expectation}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[Y\mid G_i]}.
$$

That distinction is exactly why a single period-end payment is not an exact
treatment of a mid-period default. Refining every interval into smaller
default-time buckets turns the sum into an integral. Shreve gives the finite
disjoint-event expectation rule used here[@shreve-stochastic-calculus-finance-ii; Ch. 1 §1.3, printed pp. 13–18]. Tuckman and Serrat's CDS table likewise weights
cash flows by survival/default events[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.6, printed pp. 368–370, Table 14.10 and eqs. 14.6–14.7].

## Hazard supplies the probabilities in the formulas

The lesson's [[hazard-rate]] $\lambda$ is constant, non-negative, and
measured per model-year under the [[risk-neutral-probability-measure]]
$\mathbb{Q}$. It gives

$$
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,t)}
=\exp(-\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}
\explain{payment-time}{t}),
$$

and therefore

$$
\explain{risk-neutral-probability-measure}{\mathbb{Q}}
(\explain{cds-default-time}{\tau}\in d
\explain{cds-default-integration-time}{u})
=\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}\,d
\explain{cds-default-integration-time}{u}.
$$

For an interval, integrating that density gives the familiar survival drop:

$$
\explain{interval-default-probability}{\Delta q_i^{\mathbb{Q}}}
=\exp(-\lambda t_{i-1})-\exp(-\lambda t_i).
$$

These are pricing probabilities, not forecasts. A CDS-implied hazard rate can
be risk-neutral rather than a prediction of realized defaults[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.7, printed p. 371]. The constant-hazard survival relation is given in §14.6 and
Appendix A14.1[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; printed pp. 367–368 and 505, eqs. 14.4–14.5 and A14.1–A14.2].

## What happens when default is mid-period

Suppose default occurs at $u$ in $(t_{i-1},t_i]$. The supplied
[[recovery-rate]] is $R$.

- The scheduled premium at $t_i$ is not paid, because the reference entity did
  not survive to that date.
- The buyer pays premium accrued from $t_{i-1}$ to $u$. In this model its year
  fraction is
  $\explain{cds-accrued-year-fraction-at-default}{a_i(u)}=u-t_{i-1}$.
- The seller pays protection of
  $\explain{loss-given-default}{\mathrm{LGD}}N=(1-R)N$.
- Both default-triggered amounts are discounted from the actual modeled time
  $u$, not automatically from $t_i$.

Hull explicitly notes that a final accrued premium is paid when default occurs
between scheduled dates[@hull-options-futures; §24.1, printed pp. 548–550].
The half-period and midpoint calculations in Hull and Tuckman are useful
discrete approximations[@hull-options-futures; §24.2, printed pp. 551–554, Tables 24.1–24.4][@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.6, printed pp. 368–370, Table 14.10]. Here, because the toy hazard and discount rate are constant, we
can integrate the modeled default time exactly instead.

## Exact premium-leg expectation in this model

Let the [[continuously-compounded-risk-free-rate]] be $r$, so
$D(0,u)=\exp(-ru)$. The positive scheduled-premium factor is

$$
\explain{cds-scheduled-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{sched}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\explain{cds-accrual-year-fraction}{\alpha_i}
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,t_i)}
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,t_i)}.
$$

The exact accrued-on-default factor is

$$
\explain{cds-accrued-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{accr}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}
\explain{cds-accrued-year-fraction-at-default}{a_i(u)}
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,u)}
\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}\,du.
$$

Add the factors and multiply by annual decimal spread and positive notional:

$$
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
=\explain{cds-scheduled-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{sched}}}
+\explain{cds-accrued-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{accr}}},
\qquad
\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
=\explain{cds-contract-spread}{s}
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}.
$$

A spread quoted in [[basis-point]] units per year is divided by 10,000
before entering the formula.

## Exact protection-leg expectation in this model

The positive protection magnitude is

$$
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
=\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{loss-given-default}{\mathrm{LGD}}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,u)}
\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}\,du.
$$

The expression is an expectation: each possible default-time payoff is
multiplied by its risk-neutral probability density and discounted, then the
disjoint time buckets are added.

## Closed forms and the zero-upfront par spread

For the regular model-year schedule, $\alpha_i=t_i-t_{i-1}$ and the
[[maturity-time]] is $T=t_n$. Define the
algebraic abbreviation
$\explain{cds-combined-decay-rate}{\kappa}=r+\lambda$. Then

$$
\explain{cds-scheduled-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{sched}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i\exp(-\kappa t_i),
$$

$$
\explain{cds-accrued-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{accr}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\lambda\exp(-\kappa t_{i-1})
\frac{1-\exp(-\kappa\alpha_i)(1+\kappa\alpha_i)}{\kappa^2},
$$

and the per-unit-notional protection factor is

$$
\explain{cds-protection-factor}{B_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
=\mathrm{LGD}\,\lambda
\frac{1-\exp(-\kappa T)}{\kappa}.
$$

At $\kappa=0$, the continuous limits replace the two fractions by
$\alpha_i^2/2$ and $T$, respectively. The implementation uses stable versions
of those limits.

The [[cds-par-spread]] $s^\star$ is the running spread that makes the legs
equal with no upfront amount:

$$
\explain{cds-par-spread}{s^\star}
=\frac{\explain{cds-protection-factor}{B_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}}
{\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}},
\qquad
PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}(s^\star)=PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}.
$$

Tuckman and Serrat state the equal-leg fair-spread condition and its
spread/upfront relationship[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.6, printed pp. 370–371, and Appendix A14.2, printed pp. 506–507]. The next lesson separates
this model par spread from a standard running coupon and a signed upfront fee.

## Why there is no volatility input here

Risk-neutral valuation does not make volatility disappear. A change from the
real-world measure to $\mathbb{Q}$ changes pricing probabilities (and, in common
diffusion models, drift), not the size of random fluctuations; Shreve shows the
Brownian volatility term remains after the risk-neutral change of
measure[@shreve-stochastic-calculus-finance-ii; Ch. 5 §5.2.2, printed pp. 214–217].

This particular CDS model needs no separate volatility parameter because its
risk-free rate, hazard rate, and recovery are deterministic, and both legs are
linear expectations of cash flows. If rates, hazard, or recovery were
stochastic—or if the product were a CDS option, had wrong-way risk, or had
nonlinear exposure—their distributions, dependence, and volatility could
matter. The risk-neutral measure supplies the pricing weights; the restrictive
model assumptions are what remove a separate volatility input here.

## Model boundary and review note

This is an exact valuation only inside its deliberately simplified model. It
uses model-year dates, one constant risk-neutral hazard, one deterministic
continuously compounded risk-free rate, deterministic recovery, and immediate
default-time settlement. It omits calendar schedules, day counts, business-day
rules, payment delays, term-structure calibration, auctions, deliverables,
credit-event legal definitions, counterparty risk, collateral, funding,
restructuring, and transaction costs. All materially changed content remains
\`draft\` pending independent human review.
