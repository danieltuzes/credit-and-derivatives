## What a CDS is

A credit default swap (CDS) is a bilateral derivative on a named reference
entity. The protection buyer pays a running premium to the protection seller.
If a covered credit event occurs before maturity, the seller makes a
loss-related protection payment. Unlike buying the entity's bond, entering a
CDS does not itself lend principal to the reference entity. Tuckman and Serrat
describe the fee and contingent legs in Chapter 14[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.5, printed pp. 361–362]; Hull describes the same buyer/seller cash-flow directions
and quarterly premium convention[@hull-options-futures; §24.1, printed pp. 548–550].

This lesson reports both leg present values as positive magnitudes. From the
protection buyer's perspective,

$$
\explain{cds-protection-buyer-net-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{buyer}}}
=\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
-\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}.
$$

The seller has the opposite signed value in this two-party model.

## Start from disjoint events

Let the random [[cds-default-time]] be $\tau$. Use the local
[[cds-period-index]] $i$ across the
[[number-of-cds-periods]] $n$. The schedule has dates
$0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=T$. Define the disjoint interval events

$$
\explain{cds-default-event}{G_i}
=\{\explain{payment-time}{t_{i-1}}<
\explain{cds-default-time}{\tau}\leq
\explain{payment-time}{t_i}\},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
$$

and add $\explain{cds-default-event}{G_0}=\{\tau>T\}$ for survival through
maturity. These events are
disjoint and exhaustive under the one-default assumption.

For a local [[cds-event-payoff]] $Y$ that is constant at $y_i$ on each
event,

$$
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\explain{cds-event-payoff}{Y}]
=\sum_{i=0}^{n}
\explain{cds-event-payoff-value}{y_i}\,
\explain{risk-neutral-probability-measure}{\mathbb{Q}}
(\explain{cds-default-event}{G_i}).
$$

This is the finite [[expectation]] rule from the probability lesson, now using
risk-neutral pricing weights. If $Y$ is not constant within an interval, the
correct identity is

$$
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\explain{cds-event-payoff}{Y}]
=\sum_{i=0}^{n}
\explain{risk-neutral-probability-measure}{\mathbb{Q}}
(\explain{cds-default-event}{G_i})
\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\explain{cds-event-payoff}{Y}\mid\explain{cds-default-event}{G_i}].
$$

That distinction is exactly why a single period-end payment is not an exact
treatment of a mid-period default. Refining every interval into smaller
default-time buckets turns the sum into an integral. Shreve gives the finite
disjoint-event expectation rule used here[@shreve-stochastic-calculus-finance-ii; Ch. 1 §1.3, printed pp. 13–18]. Tuckman and Serrat's CDS table likewise weights
cash flows by survival/default events[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.6, printed pp. 368–370, Table 14.10 and eqs. 14.6–14.7].

## Hazard supplies the probabilities in the formulas

Let the risk-neutral default intensity at a survived time
$\explain{cds-default-integration-time}{u}$ be
$\explain{cds-general-hazard-rate}{\lambda(u)}$ — a deterministic function of
time, measured per model-year under the [[risk-neutral-probability-measure]]
$\mathbb{Q}$, that generalizes this lesson's constant [[hazard-rate]]
$\lambda$. Its conditional-intensity meaning is unchanged from the credit
lesson's definition, only no longer assumed constant: $\lambda(u)$ is the
proportional rate at which survival is lost at $u$, given survival to $u$.
Written as a starting relation, that meaning is the differential equation

$$
-\,d\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}
=\explain{cds-general-hazard-rate}{\lambda(u)}\,
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}\,d
\explain{cds-default-integration-time}{u},
\qquad
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,0)}=1.
$$

Dividing by the (positive) survival probability turns the left side into a
derivative of its logarithm, $-d\ln
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}=
\explain{cds-general-hazard-rate}{\lambda(u)}\,d
\explain{cds-default-integration-time}{u}$. Integrating from $0$ to
$\explain{payment-time}{t}$ and using $\ln
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,0)}=\ln1=0$ gives

$$
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,t)}
=\exp\left(-\int_0^{\explain{payment-time}{t}}
\explain{cds-general-hazard-rate}{\lambda(u)}\,d
\explain{cds-default-integration-time}{u}\right).
$$

Integrating the same starting relation over one period, rather than from $0$,
gives the general interval default probability without assuming any closed
form for survival:

$$
\explain{interval-default-probability}{\Delta q_i^{\mathbb{Q}}}
=\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}
\explain{cds-general-hazard-rate}{\lambda(u)}\,
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}\,d
\explain{cds-default-integration-time}{u}
=\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,t_{i-1})}
-\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,t_i)}.
$$

This lesson's constant hazard rate [[hazard-rate]] $\lambda$ is the special
case $\lambda(u)\equiv\lambda$ for every $u$: then
$\int_0^t\lambda(u)\,du=\lambda t$, collapsing the two results above to the
familiar

$$
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,t)}=\exp(-\lambda t),
\qquad
\explain{interval-default-probability}{\Delta q_i^{\mathbb{Q}}}
=\exp(-\lambda t_{i-1})-\exp(-\lambda t_i).
$$

The premium-leg and protection-leg sections just below keep the general
$\lambda(u)$; the closed-form section after them specializes.

These are pricing probabilities, not forecasts, whether or not $\lambda$ is
held constant. A CDS-implied hazard rate can
be risk-neutral rather than a prediction of realized defaults[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.7, printed p. 371]. The constant-hazard survival relation is given in §14.6 and
Appendix A14.1[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; printed pp. 367–368 and 505, eqs. 14.4–14.5 and A14.1–A14.2].

## What happens when default is mid-period

Suppose default occurs at $u$ in $(t_{i-1},t_i]$. The supplied
[[recovery-rate]] is $R$.

- The scheduled premium at $t_i$ is not paid, because the reference entity did
  not survive to that date.
- The buyer pays premium accrued from $t_{i-1}$ to $u$. In this model its year
  fraction is
  $\explain{cds-accrued-year-fraction-at-default}{a_i(u)}=u-t_{i-1}$.
- The seller pays protection of
  $\explain{loss-given-default}{\mathrm{LGD}}N=(1-R)N$.
- Both default-triggered amounts are discounted from the actual modeled time
  $u$, not automatically from $t_i$.

Hull explicitly notes that a final accrued premium is paid when default occurs
between scheduled dates[@hull-options-futures; §24.1, printed pp. 548–550].
The half-period and midpoint calculations in Hull and Tuckman are useful
discrete approximations[@hull-options-futures; §24.2, printed pp. 551–554, Tables 24.1–24.4][@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.6, printed pp. 368–370, Table 14.10]. Here we integrate the modeled default time exactly instead, for a
general hazard rate and discount curve; the worked closed forms further below
then specialize both to this lesson's constant case.

## Exact premium-leg expectation in this model

Let $\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,u)}$ be this course's already-established,
general dated discount curve — no flat-rate assumption yet. The positive
scheduled-premium factor is

$$
\explain{cds-scheduled-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{sched}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\explain{cds-accrual-year-fraction}{\alpha_i}
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,t_i)}
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,t_i)}.
$$

The exact accrued-on-default factor is

$$
\explain{cds-accrued-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{accr}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}
\explain{cds-accrued-year-fraction-at-default}{a_i(u)}
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,u)}
\explain{cds-general-hazard-rate}{\lambda(u)}
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}\,\mathrm{d}u.
$$

Add the factors and multiply by annual decimal spread and positive notional:

$$
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
=\explain{cds-scheduled-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{sched}}}
+\explain{cds-accrued-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{accr}}},
\qquad
\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
=\explain{cds-contract-spread}{s}
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}.
\label{eq:exact-cds-premium-leg}
$$

A spread quoted in [[basis-point]] units per year is divided by 10,000
before entering [[eq-exact-cds-premium-leg]].

## Exact protection-leg expectation in this model

The positive protection magnitude is

$$
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
=\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{loss-given-default}{\mathrm{LGD}}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,u)}
\explain{cds-general-hazard-rate}{\lambda(u)}
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,u)}\,\mathrm{d}u.
\label{eq:exact-cds-protection-leg}
$$

Equation [[eq-exact-cds-protection-leg]] is an expectation: each possible default-time payoff is
multiplied by its risk-neutral probability density and discounted, then the
disjoint time buckets are added.

## Closed forms and the zero-upfront par spread

Now specialize both generalized inputs above to this lesson's worked model.
Take a constant hazard rate, $\lambda(u)\equiv\lambda$ for every $u$ — this
lesson's constant [[hazard-rate]], recovered above as a special case of
[[cds-general-hazard-rate]] — and a constant continuously compounded
risk-free rate, so that the general discount curve used in the last two
sections is $\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,u)}=\exp(-ru)$ via the lesson's
[[continuously-compounded-risk-free-rate]] $r$.

For the regular model-year schedule, $\alpha_i=t_i-t_{i-1}$ and the
[[maturity-time]] is $T=t_n$. Define the
algebraic abbreviation
$\explain{cds-combined-decay-rate}{\kappa}=r+\lambda$. Then

$$
\explain{cds-scheduled-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{sched}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i\exp(-\kappa t_i),
$$

$$
\explain{cds-accrued-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{accr}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\lambda\exp(-\kappa t_{i-1})
\frac{1-\exp(-\kappa\alpha_i)(1+\kappa\alpha_i)}{\kappa^2},
$$

and the per-unit-notional protection factor is

$$
\explain{cds-protection-factor}{B_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
=\mathrm{LGD}\,\lambda
\frac{1-\exp(-\kappa T)}{\kappa}.
$$

At $\kappa=0$, the continuous limits replace the two fractions by
$\alpha_i^2/2$ and $T$, respectively. The implementation uses stable versions
of those limits.

The [[cds-par-spread]] $s^\star$ is the running spread that makes the legs
equal with no upfront amount:

$$
\explain{cds-par-spread}{s^\star}
=\frac{\explain{cds-protection-factor}{B_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}}
{\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}},
\qquad
PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}(s^\star)=PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}.
$$

Tuckman and Serrat state the equal-leg fair-spread condition and its
spread/upfront relationship[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §14.6, printed pp. 370–371, and Appendix A14.2, printed pp. 506–507]. The next lesson separates
this model par spread from a standard running coupon and a signed upfront fee.

## Generalizing the premium leg: an extinguishing swap

This lesson uses **extinguishing swap** as a local label for the
construction below. It is not a claim that this phrase is standard market or
textbook terminology — it names, for this course only, any scheduled cash
flow that permanently stops the instant the reference entity defaults, the
same mechanism already derived above for the premium leg.

Consider the same schedule of dates $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=T$ against the same
reference entity, but replace the premium leg's one scheduled amount
$\explain{cds-contract-spread}{s}\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-accrual-year-fraction}{\alpha_i}$ at each date with an arbitrary
deterministic, signed net cash flow
$\explain{cds-extinguishing-swap-cash-flow}{c_i}$ for one named perspective —
positive received, negative paid, under this course's signed cash-flow
convention. As with the premium leg, a scheduled amount is paid only on
survival to its date, and every later scheduled amount is extinguished the
instant default occurs. This lesson keeps $c_i$ supplied and deterministic —
it is not modeled as contingent on a floating rate — and an accrued-at-default
term can be added by the same construction as the premium leg's
[[cds-accrued-premium-annuity]] if a flow accrues continuously between dates;
that term is not repeated here.

Reusing the disjoint-event expectation already used for every CDS leg on this
page, the scheduled part values by the same survival-weighted discounting
that gave $A_0^{\mathrm{sched}}$ above:

$$
\explain{cds-extinguishing-swap-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{ext}}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\explain{cds-extinguishing-swap-cash-flow}{c_i}\,
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,t_i)}\,
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,t_i)}.
$$

Setting $\explain{cds-extinguishing-swap-cash-flow}{c_i}=
\explain{cds-contract-spread}{s}\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-accrual-year-fraction}{\alpha_i}$ for every $i$ recovers the
premium leg's scheduled-payment term $A_0^{\mathrm{sched}}$ exactly: the
premium leg is the special case of an extinguishing swap whose schedule is
one running spread paid on a fixed notional.

PV_0^{\mathrm{ext}}
=30{,}000(0.970445534)+(-12{,}000)(0.941764534)
\approx\text{USD }17{,}812.19.
$$

Both terms reuse exactly the $D(0,t_i)S(0,t_i)$ factors above; only the
signed schedule $c_i$ changed from the premium leg's $sN\alpha_i$.

## Why there is no volatility input here

Risk-neutral valuation does not make volatility disappear. A change from the
real-world measure to $\mathbb{Q}$ changes pricing probabilities (and, in common
diffusion models, drift), not the size of random fluctuations; Shreve shows the
Brownian volatility term remains after the risk-neutral change of
measure[@shreve-stochastic-calculus-finance-ii; Ch. 5 §5.2.2, printed pp. 214–217].

This particular CDS model needs no separate volatility parameter because its
risk-free rate, hazard rate, and recovery are deterministic, and both legs are
linear expectations of cash flows. If rates, hazard, or recovery were
stochastic—or if the product were a CDS option, had wrong-way risk, or had
nonlinear exposure—their distributions, dependence, and volatility could
matter. The risk-neutral measure supplies the pricing weights; the restrictive
model assumptions are what remove a separate volatility input here.

## Model boundary and review note

This is an exact valuation only inside its deliberately simplified model. The
premium and protection legs are derived for a general, possibly time-varying
risk-neutral hazard rate and a general dated discount curve; the worked
closed forms, numeric examples, explorer, and assessment all use this
lesson's narrower special case of one constant risk-neutral hazard and one
deterministic continuously compounded risk-free rate, with deterministic
recovery and immediate default-time settlement. The extinguishing swap is
this lesson's own construction, generalizing the premium leg's schedule
rather than a named market instrument. The model omits calendar schedules,
day counts, business-day rules, payment delays, term-structure calibration,
auctions, deliverables, credit-event legal definitions, counterparty risk,
collateral, funding, restructuring, and transaction costs. All materially
changed content remains \`draft\` pending independent human review.
