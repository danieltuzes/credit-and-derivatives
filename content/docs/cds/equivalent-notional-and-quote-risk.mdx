## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- calculate a CDS position's signed value change for a one basis point rise in
  the liquid tenor's quote, re-solving the hazard rate rather than holding the
  premium annuity fixed;
- explain why, in a one-factor model, risk measured against the
  market-standard quote, the upfront, or the par spread is the same risk;
- calculate the equivalent notional and side of the most liquid contract that
  offsets a position, and its equivalent ratio;
- state what an equivalent-ratio hedge does not offset.

## One market level moves everything

The preceding lessons value every contract on a reference entity off one flat
[[hazard-rate]] $\lambda$, and the quote lesson showed how the market level
sets it: the [[cds-market-standard-quote]] $s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}$ of the standard
contract is treated as a par spread and $\lambda$ is the root of
[[cds/market-standard-quote-and-upfront#eq-cds-msq-calibration]]. Take that
contract to be the most liquid one, with the local
[[cds-liquid-tenor-maturity]] $T_L$, in practice usually five years
[@hull-options-futures; Ch. 24 §24.1, printed p. 549]
[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 14 §14.5, printed p. 365].

A position is one contract on the same name with its own maturity, fixed
coupon, and positive local [[cds-position-notional]] $N_{\mathrm{pos}}$.
Its legs are the exact default-time expectations of the legs lesson, evaluated
at the same $\lambda$. Let the local [[cds-position-side]] $\sigma$ be $+1$
for bought protection and $-1$ for sold protection. The local
[[cds-position-value]] is

$$
\explain{cds-position-value}{V^{\mathrm{pos}}}
=\explain{cds-position-side}{\sigma}
\left(
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
-\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
\right),
\label{eq:cds-position-value}
$$

the protection-buyer value of the legs lesson with the sign of the side
attached. Hull marks a CDS to market in the same way, with opposite signs for
buyer and seller [@hull-options-futures; Ch. 24 §24.2, printed pp. 553-554].
Any upfront exchanged at inception is a sunk cash flow and is left out of
$V^{\mathrm{pos}}$; it does not change any sensitivity.

For the liquid contract itself, the local [[cds-liquid-unit-value]] is the
buyer value of one unit of notional,

$$
\explain{cds-liquid-unit-value}{v_L}
=\frac{
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
(\explain{cds-liquid-tenor-maturity}{T_L})
}{
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
}
-\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
(\explain{cds-liquid-tenor-maturity}{T_L}),
\label{eq:cds-liquid-unit-value}
$$

which is exactly the fair upfront fraction of the quote lesson, so at the
current market level it equals the liquid contract's upfront per unit
notional [@hull-options-futures; Ch. 24 §24.4, printed p. 557].

Because $\lambda$ is the only state variable, every quotation of the liquid
contract is a function of it. Write the local [[cds-liquid-quotation]] $q$
for any one of them: the market-standard quote, the upfront fraction, or the
par spread. Each is strictly increasing in $\lambda$ in this model, so each is
invertible, and "the quote moved" means "the hazard rate moved" whichever
quotation the desk marks. There is one risk factor here, seen through three
scales. In this flat-hazard, flat-rate model the par spread is even the same at
every maturity, so a position's par spread and the liquid quote are literally
one number.

## Risky DV01 by bump and reprice

Tuckman and Serrat define the risky DV01 of a CDS as the change in its value
for a one basis point change in the CDS spread, computed by finding the hazard
rate that moves the spread by one basis point and revaluing the contract
[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 14 §14.10, printed p. 379]. This lesson
uses that bump-and-reprice definition with the local [[cds-quote-bump]]
$h=0.0001$ per year, one [[basis-point]] of market-standard quote.

Write $\lambda(s_{\mathrm{MSQ}})$ for the hazard rate re-solved from a quote.
The local [[cds-position-quote-sensitivity]] is

$$
\explain{cds-position-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{\mathrm{pos}}}
=\explain{cds-position-value}{V^{\mathrm{pos}}}
\!\left(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}(\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}+\explain{cds-quote-bump}{h})\right)
-\explain{cds-position-value}{V^{\mathrm{pos}}}
\!\left(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}(\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}})\right),
\label{eq:cds-risky-dv01}
$$

and the local [[cds-liquid-unit-quote-sensitivity]] is the same difference
for one unit of bought liquid protection,

$$
\explain{cds-liquid-unit-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{L}}
=\explain{cds-liquid-unit-value}{v_L}
\!\left(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}(\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}+\explain{cds-quote-bump}{h})\right)
-\explain{cds-liquid-unit-value}{v_L}
\!\left(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}(\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}})\right).
\label{eq:cds-liquid-unit-dv01}
$$

Three points about the definition matter in practice.

- **Both legs move.** A higher hazard rate raises the protection leg and
  lowers the premium annuity, so the sensitivity is not the annuity times one
  basis point. Holding the annuity fixed is the shortcut the quote lesson
  warned against.
- **The sign belongs to the side.** Bought protection gains when the quote
  rises, so its sensitivity is positive; sold protection has the opposite
  sign. $\explain{cds-liquid-unit-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{L}}$ is
  defined for bought protection and is positive.
- **It is a finite bump.** The value is a curved function of the quote, so a
  one basis point bump and the derivative differ slightly; the model boundary
  returns to this.

## The liquid-tenor equivalent

A hedge that removes the quote risk is the liquid contract in the amount that
makes the hedged sensitivity zero. With the local
[[cds-equivalent-notional]] $N_{\mathrm{eq}}$ of bought liquid protection,

$$
\explain{cds-position-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{\mathrm{pos}}}
+\explain{cds-equivalent-notional}{N_{\mathrm{eq}}}\,
\explain{cds-liquid-unit-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{L}}
=0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\explain{cds-equivalent-notional}{N_{\mathrm{eq}}}
=-\frac{
\explain{cds-position-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{\mathrm{pos}}}
}{
\explain{cds-liquid-unit-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{L}}
}.
\label{eq:cds-equivalent-notional}
$$

A positive $N_{\mathrm{eq}}$ means buy protection in the liquid tenor; a
negative one means sell it. Because
$\explain{cds-liquid-unit-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{L}}$ is positive, the hedge
always takes the side opposite to the sign of the position's sensitivity. The
local [[cds-equivalent-ratio]] states the size relative to the position,

$$
\explain{cds-equivalent-ratio}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}
=\frac{
\lvert\explain{cds-equivalent-notional}{N_{\mathrm{eq}}}\rvert
}{
\explain{cds-position-notional}{N_{\mathrm{pos}}}
}.
\label{eq:cds-equivalent-ratio}
$$

This is the DV01-neutral hedge of the bond lessons in Tuckman and Serrat,
where the face amount of a liquid hedge is minus the ratio of the two DV01s
times the position amount, and the instrument with the larger sensitivity is
traded in the smaller quantity
[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 4 §4.3, printed pp. 110-111, eqs. 4.6-4.9].
The phrases **equivalent notional** and **equivalent ratio** are this course's
labels for the hedge amount and its ratio (`NEEDS_SOURCE` for desk usage; the
registered sources define the DV01 hedge but not these names).

**Which quote risk?** A desk may mark the position's risk against the
market-standard quote, against the upfront, or against a par spread. In this
model it does not matter. In the limit of a small bump, both sensitivities are
derivatives with respect to the same quotation $q$, and the chain rule through
the one state variable gives

$$
\explain{cds-equivalent-notional}{N_{\mathrm{eq}}}
=-\frac{
d\explain{cds-position-value}{V^{\mathrm{pos}}}/d\explain{cds-liquid-quotation}{q}
}{
d\explain{cds-liquid-unit-value}{v_L}/d\explain{cds-liquid-quotation}{q}
}
=-\frac{
\left(d\explain{cds-position-value}{V^{\mathrm{pos}}}/d\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}\right)
\left(d\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}/d\explain{cds-liquid-quotation}{q}\right)
}{
\left(d\explain{cds-liquid-unit-value}{v_L}/d\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}\right)
\left(d\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}/d\explain{cds-liquid-quotation}{q}\right)
}
=-\frac{
d\explain{cds-position-value}{V^{\mathrm{pos}}}/d\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}
}{
d\explain{cds-liquid-unit-value}{v_L}/d\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}
}.
\label{eq:cds-equivalent-invariance}
$$

The factor that converts hazard into quotation cancels. MSQ risk, upfront risk,
and par spread risk are one risk, and the same equivalent notional offsets all
three. The sensitivities themselves do differ across quotations, because they
are measured per unit of different things; the hedge amount does not. With a
finite bump the three ratios differ only through curvature, as the worked
example shows.

[[eq-cds-liquid-unit-dv01]],

$$
\explain{cds-position-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{\mathrm{pos}}}
=363{,}240.36-357{,}482.24
=\text{USD }5{,}758.12
\text{ per basis point},
$$

\explain{cds-liquid-unit-quote-sensitivity}{\mathrm{DV01}^{L}}
=0.000434070474
\text{ per unit notional per basis point}.

$\explain{cds-equivalent-ratio}{\rho_{\mathrm{eq}}}=1.3265$. The seven-year

## What an equivalent-ratio hedge does not do

The hedge cancels one derivative at one market level under one model. It
leaves at least the following exposures, each of which a desk manages
separately.

- **Default itself.** The seven-year hedge above sells protection on
  USD 13,265,398 against bought protection on USD 10,000,000. On an immediate
  default with 40% recovery, the position receives
  $\explain{loss-given-default}{\mathrm{LGD}}\times10{,}000{,}000=\text{USD }6{,}000{,}000$
  while the hedge pays $0.6\times13{,}265{,}398.37=\text{USD }7{,}959{,}239.02$, a
  net loss of about USD 1,959,239 before accrued premium. Matching quote
  sensitivity is not matching the default payoff; the jump-to-default exposure
  of a hedged book must be measured on its own.
- **Curve shape.** The model has one hazard rate. Under the fitted curve of
  the credit-curve lesson, a seven-year position also depends on segments the
  five-year contract does not see, and a shift of the five-year quote alone
  would move only part of the position. Bucketing the sensitivity by tenor is
  the standard extension (`NEEDS_SOURCE`: the registered sources describe
  single-factor DV01 hedging only).
- **Curvature and the bump.** Both sensitivities change as the quote moves, so
  the hedge drifts and must be rebalanced; Tuckman and Serrat make the same
  point for bond DV01 hedges [@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 4 §4.2, printed p. 109 (DV01 as a local measure)].
- **Everything held fixed.** Recovery, the discount curve, and the accrual
  and settlement conventions were held constant. A change in any of them
  moves both contracts differently.
- **Costs.** The hedge crosses the liquid contract's bid-offer, and the
  position's own liquidity is why the hedge is not simply the offsetting
  trade in the position's tenor [@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 14 §14.6, printed pp. 366-367 (unwinding through the on-the-run contract and managing the maturity and spread mismatch)].

editorial and quantitative review. Inputs are synthetic. The sensitivity is a
one basis point bump of the liquid tenor's market-standard quote under one
flat hazard rate, one deterministic discount rate, one deterministic recovery
