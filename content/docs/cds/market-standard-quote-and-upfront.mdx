## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- distinguish a conventional spread quote from the contract's fixed running
  coupon;
- identify which quantity is supplied and which is solved in each quotation
  direction;
- calculate the signed upfront amount that makes protection-buyer inception
  value zero;
- reverse the simplified conversion without incorrectly freezing a
  quote-dependent premium annuity.

## Three numbers with three different jobs

Standardized CDS quotation separates a fixed running coupon from a
market-determined upfront amount. Textbook treatments describe converting a
quoted or par spread into the value of a contract with a fixed coupon, while
the official converter specification accepts either spread or upfront and
returns the other. [@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 14 §14.6, printed pp. 366-371; Appendix A14.2, printed pp. 506-507, eqs. A14.5-A14.7]
[@hull-options-futures; Ch. 24 §24.4, printed pp. 556-557]
[@isda-cds-standard-model; Standard CDS Contract Converter Specification, version May 5, 2009, Functionality and Specification, printed p. 1]

This lesson uses **market-standard quote**, abbreviated **MSQ**, as a local
label for that conventional quoted spread. The books and official
specification more commonly say _quoted spread_, _conventional spread_, or
simply _spread_; this page does not claim that “MSQ” is a universal acronym.

The three quantities have distinct jobs, but they are not three independent
inputs:

- the [[cds-market-standard-quote]]
  $\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}$ is treated as a
  zero-upfront [[cds-par-spread]] and calibrates this lesson's flat
  [[hazard-rate]] $\lambda$;
- the [[cds-standard-coupon]]
  $\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}$ determines the running
  premium cash flows;
- the [[cds-upfront-amount]]
  $\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}$ bridges the value difference at
  inception.

“Upfront fee” is the usual phrase intended here; this lesson prefers
**upfront amount** because the amount is signed. Its convention is

- $U_0>0$: the protection buyer pays;
- $U_0=0$: no upfront cash changes hands in the toy model;
- $U_0<0$: the protection buyer receives $-U_0$.

Rates use decimals per year in formulas. Convert a [[basis-point]] quote
to decimal form by dividing by 10,000.

## First decide what is supplied and what is solved

The equation connecting par spread and hazard can be used in opposite
directions. Calling everything an “input” hides that distinction.

| Calculation      | Supplied                                                                                           | Solved or calculated                                  |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Model par spread | Hazard rate, recovery, discount input, maturity, and cash-flow conventions                         | Par spread $s^{\star}$                                |
| MSQ to upfront   | Market quote $s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}$, standard coupon $c_{\mathrm{std}}$, and the converter conventions | Flat hazard rate $\lambda$, then signed upfront $U_0$ |
| Upfront to MSQ   | Market upfront $U_0$, standard coupon $c_{\mathrm{std}}$, and the converter conventions            | Flat hazard rate $\lambda$, then $s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}$   |

If the hazard rate is already supplied, the par spread is the output:

$$
\explain{cds-par-spread}{s^{\star}}
=
\frac{
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
}{
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
}.
\label{eq:cds-par-spread-from-hazard}
$$

If the MSQ is supplied instead, [[eq-cds-par-spread-from-hazard]] is a
calibration equation: the quoted spread is the input and the flat hazard rate
is the unknown. If the upfront is supplied, the upfront balance is the
calibration equation. The market participant or price source supplies the
market level; the standard converter specifies how spread and upfront are
translated under common assumptions. [@isda-cds-standard-model; Standard CDS Contract Converter Specification, version May 5, 2009, Functionality and Specification, printed pp. 1 and 4]

Consequently, do not fix both $s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}$ and $\lambda$ independently.
Once the other model inputs are fixed, they must satisfy the zero-upfront
condition together.

## Why one upfront amount is enough at inception

At a fixed set of model inputs, all future protection cash flows have already
been summarized by one positive
[[cds-protection-leg-present-value]] magnitude, and all fixed-coupon cash
flows by one positive [[cds-premium-leg-present-value]] magnitude. Their
difference is one currency amount. One cash transfer at time zero can
therefore offset that difference exactly:

$$
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
=
\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
+\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}.
\label{eq:cds-upfront-balance}
$$

Equivalently, protection-buyer inception value after the signed upfront is

$$
0=
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
-\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
-\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}.
$$

This is a value identity, not a claim that the CDS becomes riskless. After
inception its value can change when credit, interest-rate, recovery, liquidity,
or other inputs change.

## Risk-neutral does not mean risk-free

The calibration uses **risk-neutral pricing probabilities**, not necessarily
real-world default forecasts. Under risk-neutral valuation, probabilities are
pricing weights chosen consistently with traded prices and risk-free
discounting; “risk-free” describes the discount input, not the probability
measure. The hazard rate inferred from prices can therefore differ from an
estimated real-world default intensity. [@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 7 §7.3, printed pp. 182-184, eqs. 7.7-7.8; Ch. 14 §14.7, printed p. 371]
[@hull-options-futures; Ch. 23 §23.5, printed pp. 528-530]

It would be inaccurate to say that changing probability measure makes
volatility disappear. This particular converter has no separate volatility
input because its discount rate, hazard rate, recovery, and schedule are
deterministic and its leg values are linear expectations of one-default cash
flows. A model with stochastic rates, default intensity, recovery,
wrong-way dependence, or CDS options can require volatility and correlation
inputs. The risk-neutral measure supplies pricing weights; it does not erase
those risks.

## Convert MSQ to signed upfront

The conversion must use one internally consistent model. For each candidate
constant hazard rate, calculate the positive
[[cds-premium-annuity]]
$\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})$
and protection-leg value. First solve the zero-upfront quote condition

$$
\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
=
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}).
\label{eq:cds-msq-calibration}
$$

That root solve makes $\lambda$ the flat risk-neutral hazard rate implied by
the MSQ under this lesson's schedule, discount, recovery, and default-time
accrual assumptions. The fixed-coupon premium leg is then

$$
\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
(\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}};\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
=
\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}).
\label{eq:cds-fixed-coupon-premium}
$$

Substitute [[eq-cds-msq-calibration]] and [[eq-cds-fixed-coupon-premium]] into
the balance [[eq-cds-upfront-balance]]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}
&=
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
-
\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})\\
&=
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
\left(
\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}
-\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cds-upfront-from-msq}
$$

The substitution in [[eq-cds-upfront-from-msq]] is valid only because every
term uses the same model and calibrated hazard rate. Since the premium annuity and notional
are positive, the sign is easy to check: an MSQ above the fixed coupon gives a
positive buyer-paid upfront; an MSQ below it gives a negative upfront received
by the buyer.

## Convert signed upfront back to MSQ

Given a signed upfront amount, do not generally rearrange the final line while
silently holding the annuity fixed. In this flat-hazard converter, both the
protection value and premium annuity change with the unknown hazard rate.
Instead use the hazard equation

$$
\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}
=
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
-
\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
\label{eq:cds-upfront-to-hazard}
$$

Solve [[eq-cds-upfront-to-hazard]] for a non-negative $\lambda$, then
substitute that solution into

$$
\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}
=
\frac{
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
}{
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
}.
\label{eq:cds-hazard-to-msq}
$$

Equation [[eq-cds-hazard-to-msq]] then reports the corresponding MSQ.

If a curve and its annuity have already been calibrated and are deliberately
held fixed, the local algebraic shortcut is

$$
\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}
=
\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}
+
\frac{
\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}
}{
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
}.
$$

That shortcut is not the full upfront-to-quote conversion when the quote
itself determines $\lambda$ and therefore changes the denominator.

## When MSQ and par spread are equal

Within one internally consistent risk-neutral model, they are equal. After the
converter has inferred one $\lambda$ from either the spread or the upfront,
the following quantities are the same:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}
&=
\explain{cds-par-spread}{s^{\star}}\\
&=
\frac{
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
}{
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
}\\
&=
\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}
+
\frac{
\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}
}{
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cds-msq-par-equality}
$$

The equality in [[eq-cds-msq-par-equality]] is a risk-neutral pricing identity.
No real-world default probability enters it. The equality requires the same
pricing probabilities, discount inputs, recovery, schedule, default-accrual
treatment, and settlement convention in every term.

The phrase **par spread from another curve** needs more care. The observed
standard-coupon trade supplies the one combined price

$$
\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}
=
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
-
\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}
\explain{cds-notional}{N}
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}.
$$

That price does not separately observe the protection value and the premium
annuity. The standard converter resolves the separation by imposing its
constant-hazard conversion model. A different risk-neutral term structure can
reprice the same fixed-coupon/upfront contract while assigning a different
premium annuity, and therefore a different par coupon to a hypothetical
zero-upfront contract. The official converter itself distinguishes its single
constant hazard rate from a term structure used in a fuller credit-curve
model. [@isda-cds-standard-model; Standard CDS Examples, version October 26, 2012, printed p. 2 (the converter uses a single flat hazard rate rather than a term structure of flat spreads)]

This difference is not by itself an arbitrage: the observed fixed-coupon CDS
and the hypothetical zero-upfront CDS have different premium cash flows. No
arbitrage requires identical tradable cash flows to have the same price. If a
zero-upfront CDS paying $s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}$ were itself executable on otherwise
identical terms, however, a curve calibrated consistently to that traded claim
would have to give it zero value. [@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 1 §§1.3-1.4, printed pp. 53-57 (law of one price, replication, and arbitrage enforcement)]

Consider a synthetic five-year quarterly CDS with notional
$\explain{cds-notional}{N}=\text{USD }10{,}000{,}000$, 2% continuously
compounded risk-free rate, 40% recovery, MSQ
$\explain{cds-market-standard-quote}{s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}}=0.015$ per year (150
basis points per year), and fixed coupon
$\explain{cds-standard-coupon}{c_{\mathrm{std}}}=0.010$ per year (100 basis
points per year).

Under the lesson's exact-default-time flat-hazard model, the quote calibration
gives

$$
\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda}=0.024937617\text{ per year},
\qquad
\explain{cds-premium-annuity}{A_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}(\explain{hazard-rate}{\lambda})
=4.466915290\text{ model-years}.
$$

Because the calibrated quote is a par spread, protection value is

$$
\explain{cds-protection-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prot}}}
=0.015(10{,}000{,}000)(4.466915290)
=\text{USD }670{,}037.29.
$$

The fixed-coupon premium value is

$$
\explain{cds-premium-leg-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{prem}}}
=0.010(10{,}000{,}000)(4.466915290)
=\text{USD }446{,}691.53.
$$

Therefore

$$
\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}
=670{,}037.29-446{,}691.53
=\text{USD }223{,}345.76.
$$

The amount is positive, so the protection buyer pays it. Substitution gives
$670{,}037.29-446{,}691.53-223{,}345.76=0$ up to displayed rounding.

  <CompactExample label="Why reverse conversion is a solve">

Suppose the same contract instead arrives with signed upfront
$\explain{cds-upfront-amount}{U_0}=\text{USD }223{,}345.76$ and no MSQ. A
solver varies the flat $\lambda$ and recomputes both protection value and the
premium annuity until their fixed-coupon difference matches that upfront.

The root is approximately $\lambda=0.024937617$ per year. Only after finding
that root does the ratio of protection value to notional times premium annuity
return $s_{\mathrm{MSQ}}\approx0.015$, or 150 basis points per year.

Using $4.466915290$ as though it were independent of the unknown quote would
happen to reproduce the answer only because that annuity was already computed
at the answer's hazard rate.

## What a real standard converter adds

The official converter is date- and convention-aware: its specified inputs
include trade and maturity dates, notional, standard coupon, recovery,
discount inputs, and either spread or upfront; its outputs include the other
quotation and a cash-settlement amount.
[@isda-cds-standard-model; Standard CDS Contract Converter Specification, version May 5, 2009, Functionality and Specification, printed p. 1]

This lesson is not an implementation of that standard model. It uses
model-year periods and a single flat hazard rate. A production conversion also
has to respect calendar schedules, day-count and business-day conventions,
curve construction and interpolation, settlement timing, accrued amount, and
other contract details. The simple identities remain useful for understanding
why quote, coupon, and upfront differ, but the displayed number is not a trade
cash-settlement instruction.

## Model boundary and review note

This AI-assisted lesson and all cited locators remain `draft` pending human
editorial and quantitative review. Inputs are synthetic. The converter assumes
one deterministic discount rate, one deterministic recovery rate, one
constant risk-neutral hazard rate, regular model-year periods, exact
default-time accrued premium inside that toy schedule, and an upfront payment
at time zero. It omits the contractual and numerical machinery listed above,
as well as stochastic rates, stochastic intensity or recovery, volatility and
correlation calibration, counterparty risk, collateral, funding, liquidity,
and transaction costs.
