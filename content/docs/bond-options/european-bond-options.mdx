## The standalone contract

A European bond call gives the holder the right, but not the obligation, to
buy a named bond at [[option-expiry-time]]. A put gives the right to sell.
This is not the issuer call feature inside a callable bond; the party holding
the right and the surrounding instrument are different.

## Align cash-price units before payoff

The local [[bond-option-expiry-value]]
$B_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{dirty}}$ and [[option-strike-price]] $K$
must use the same dirty cash basis. If a contract states a clean strike, add
exercise-date [[accrued-interest]] before comparison.

For the lesson's exercise immediately after any expiry-date coupon, the call
and put payoffs are

$$
\explain{bond-call-expiry-payoff}{X^{B,\mathrm{call}}_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}}
=\max\left(
\explain{bond-option-expiry-value}{B_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{dirty}}}
-\explain{option-strike-price}{K},0\right),
\label{eq:bond-call-expiry-payoff}
$$

$$
\explain{bond-put-expiry-payoff}{X^{B,\mathrm{put}}_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}}
=\max\left(
\explain{option-strike-price}{K}
-\explain{bond-option-expiry-value}{B_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{dirty}}},0\right).
\label{eq:bond-put-expiry-payoff}
$$

The lattice first produces expiry bond values. Apply
[[eq-bond-call-expiry-payoff]] or [[eq-bond-put-expiry-payoff]] at those nodes,
then apply backward induction to obtain the [[lattice-node-value]].

## Worked example

Suppose the supplied one-period lattice gives dirty expiry bond values of USD
90 and USD 110, strike USD 100, risk-neutral up weight 0.60, and discount
factor 0.95. Applying [[eq-bond-call-expiry-payoff]] and one backward step gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\explain{bond-call-expiry-payoff}{X_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{B,\mathrm{call},\mathrm{down}}}
&=\max(90-100,0)=0,\\
\explain{bond-call-expiry-payoff}{X_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{B,\mathrm{call},\mathrm{up}}}
&=\max(110-100,0)=10,\\
\explain{lattice-node-value}{V_0}
&=0.95\left[(1-0.60)(0)+0.60(10)\right]\\
&=5.70\ \text{USD per option}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The tested domain calculation composes `europeanOptionPayoff` with
`backwardInductionValue` for the same terminal-payoff and discounting steps.

## Coupon and exercise ordering

An expiry-date coupon is paid before exercise in this slice, so the expiry
bond value is ex-coupon. A contract exercising before the coupon or transferring
coupon entitlement would require different terminal inputs.

The bond-option definition and the need to distinguish cash/dirty from
quoted/clean strikes follow Hull, Chapter 28 §28.1 [@hull-options-futures].

subsequent issuer-default state can remain explicit.
