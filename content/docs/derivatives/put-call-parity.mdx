## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- show that long call plus short put has a forward-style expiry payoff;
- state the exact contract terms that must match;
- solve a missing European call or put value using discounted strike and known income.

## Match the expiry cash flows

For the same [[derivative-underlying-value]],
[[option-strike-price]] $K$, and [[option-expiry-time]]
$T_{\mathrm{opt}}$, the holder payoffs satisfy this identity in every state at
expiry:

$$
\max\!\left(S_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}-K,0\right)
-\max\!\left(K-S_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}},0\right)
=S_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}-K.
$$

The left side is long call plus short put. The right side is the payoff of a
long forward-style exchange at strike $K$. This is an identity in every expiry
state, not a forecast or an average.

## Present-value parity

Let local [[parity-income-present-value]] be $I_0$. The local
[[prepaid-forward-value]] is
$\widetilde{F}_{0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}}=S_0-I_0$. Applying the law of one price to
the matched portfolios, with [[call-option-value]] $c_0$,
[[put-option-value]] $p_0$, and the strike-payment [[discount-factor]]
$D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}})$, gives put-call parity in present-value form:

$$
c_0-p_0
=\widetilde{F}_{0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}}
-K D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}).
$$

The prepaid forward value is the discounted [[forward-price]]
$F_{0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}}$ for delivery at option expiry:

$$
\widetilde{F}_{0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}}
=D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}})F_{0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}}.
$$

Here $F_{0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}}$ is the current forward price, not the fixed
delivery price of an existing forward. Substituting it into the present-value
form gives put-call parity in forward form:

$$
c_0-p_0
=D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}})
\left(F_{0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}}-K\right).
$$

Hull derives put-call parity by comparing the expiry values of two portfolios
and invoking no arbitrage[@hull-options-futures; Ch. 10 §10.4, printed pp. 221-225, European put-call parity and arbitrage portfolios].

For $c_0=8$, $\widetilde{F}_{0,T_{\mathrm{opt}}}=97$, $K=100$, and
$D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}})=0.95$, the present-value form gives the put value:

$$
p_0=8-97+100(0.95)=6.
$$

## When put-call parity does not apply

The equality needs the same underlying, strike, expiry, exercise style,
quantity, income treatment, and settlement basis. American exercise adds a
choice of exercise time. A knockout triggered by default changes the cash
flows in the default state. If both options terminate on issuer default, the
call payoff minus the put payoff is zero in that state. So the matching
forward-style claim must also terminate on issuer default. The formula for
options without a knockout does not apply unchanged.

## Model boundary and review note

Put-call parity does not depend on an option pricing model. It still relies on
the stated assumptions about trading, funding, income, and matching contract
terms. Put-call parity does not give an option price by itself, and it contains
no volatility model.

The parity functions are implemented in a pure tested domain module. Sources, notation,
examples, code, and answer keys remain `draft` pending independent human review.
