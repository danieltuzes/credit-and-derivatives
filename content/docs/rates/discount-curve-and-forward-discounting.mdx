## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to read a deterministic discount curve,
state its interpolation boundary, and calculate a discount factor between two
future dates.

## A curve is a dated collection, not one yield

In the earlier lessons, the [[discount-factor]] $D(0,t)$ gave the value at
[[valuation-time]] of a deterministic payment at one [[payment-time]]. A
discount curve gives the discount factor for many payment times. Each node of
the curve is one payment time with its discount factor. Every node must have
the same currency, the same valuation date, and the same collateral and funding
basis. Nodes with different bases do not form one consistent curve.

The curve in this lesson accepts only positive discount factors at its nodes,
and it sets $D(0,0)=1$. Between two nodes, the logarithm of the discount factor
is interpolated linearly. The curve is not extrapolated beyond its final node.
Log-linear interpolation is a convention of this lesson. The lesson does not
claim that it is the only correct interpolation.

A discount factor can be larger than one. For example, $D(0,1)=1.01$ implies a
negative continuously compounded one-year zero rate. The curve requires
positive discount factors, but it does not require discount factors below one.

## Discount between future times

Let the local [[forward-discount-start-time]] $t$ be earlier than the local
[[forward-discount-end-time]] $T$. The [[forward-discount-factor]] is defined
as the ratio of the two discount factors from valuation time:

$$
\explain{forward-discount-factor}{Z(t,T)}
=\frac{\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,T)}}
{\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,t)}}.
\label{eq:forward-discount-factor}
$$

Its unit is currency at time $t$ per unit of currency at time $T$. If
$D(0,1)=0.98$ and $D(0,3)=0.94$, the forward discount factor from year 1 to
year 3 is:

$$
Z(1,3)=\frac{0.94}{0.98}=0.9591836735.
$$

The forward discount factor has its own symbol, $Z$. The symbol $D$ keeps its
meaning: a discount factor from valuation time.

The ratio in [[eq-forward-discount-factor]] is needed for multi-period
valuation. Each lattice node needs the discount factor over its next period. A
discount factor from time zero is not the correct discount factor for that
period.

## Scope boundary

The curve is an input. It is deterministic and not calibrated. Later lessons
with state-dependent discounting give a one-period discount factor at each
node. They do not assume that today's forward discount factor is realized in
every future state.
