## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- explain why a fixed positive-cash-flow bond's price falls when yield rises;
- hold contract cash flows fixed while changing only the discounting input;
- recognize that the price-yield relationship is curved rather than linear.

## Direction comes from every denominator

Write the [[bond-payment-frequency]] as $m_{\mathrm B}$. On this page,
$y$ is the shorter functional-argument form of the
[[yield-to-maturity]] $y^{(m_{\mathrm B})}$. Write the
[[price-yield-curve]] as $P(y)$.

Use the [[bond-payment-index]] $k$ to select one of the
[[number-of-bond-payments]] $n$, and write each promised positive
[[bond-cash-flow]] as $CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}$. Then

$$
P(y)=
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}}
{\left(1+y/m_{\mathrm B}\right)^k}.
$$

Within the stated non-negative-yield domain, if yield rises while every cash
flow stays fixed, every positive denominator increases. Every discounted
contribution decreases, so [[bond-price]] decreases. The shock table also
assumes the new yield remains in that domain.

Define the local [[yield-shock]] $\Delta y$ as new yield minus old
yield, and define the local [[price-change]] $\Delta P$ as new price
minus old price.

| Yield shock  | Denominators | Price change |
| ------------ | ------------ | ------------ |
| $\Delta y>0$ | Increase     | $\Delta P<0$ |
| $\Delta y<0$ | Decrease     | $\Delta P>0$ |

This statement is conditional on the promised cash flows being held fixed.

For a five-year bond with a 5% annual coupon rate, semiannual payments, and face

| -------------------: | -----------------: |

  </CompactExample>

  <CompactExample label="Equal shocks, unequal moves">

Starting from 5% yield:

- a fall to 4% raises price from 100.0000 to 104.4913, a gain of 4.4913;
- a rise to 6% lowers price from 100.0000 to 95.7349, a loss of 4.2651.

The shock sizes are equal, but the absolute price changes differ. This is the
qualitative curvature competency. Duration, DV01, and convexity are not yet
being calculated.

## Model boundary and review note

The plotted curve is a toy yield summary, not a credit curve or recommendation.
It omits accrued interest, term-structure effects, default, recovery, liquidity,
tax, funding, and embedded options.

The inverse direction and the curvature both follow Tuckman &
Serrat[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §4.1, "Price-Rate Curves", Figure 4.2, prices decrease as rates rise; §4.5, "Convexity", price-rate curves of coupon bonds are convex]; Hull notes the same negative price-yield
relationship[@hull-options-futures; §4.8, Duration]. FINRA states
plainly that price and yield are inversely
related[@finra-bond-yield; "Price and yield" section]. The lesson stays
`draft` pending human confirmation of the printed locators.
