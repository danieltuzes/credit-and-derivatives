## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the terms that determine a toy fixed-rate bond's payments;
- calculate the level coupon amount;
- construct the complete promised cash-flow schedule.

## What a bond is

A bond is a debt claim. The issuer is the borrower; the bondholder is the
lender who owns the claim. In the fixed-rate example used here, the issuer
promises a sequence of coupon payments and repayment of principal at maturity.
The price paid for the bond is a different quantity from the promised cash
flows.

The word “promises” is important. A contract can specify an amount without a
guarantee that the issuer pays it. This first bond lesson constructs the
contractual schedule. The credit-risk lessons introduce default probability and
recovery later. Tuckman and Serrat define a coupon bond by its coupon rate, its
maturity, and its face amount (also called the par amount or the principal
amount). They show the coupons and the principal on the cash-flow timeline[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; Ch. 1 §1.1, printed p. 50 and Table 1.1].

## Read the contract before valuing it

Write the simplified contract's [[face-value]] as $F$, its
[[annual-coupon-rate]] as $c$, its [[bond-payment-frequency]] as
$m_{\mathrm B}$, and its [[maturity-time]] as $T$. Write the
[[number-of-bond-payments]] as $n$ and index those dates with the
[[bond-payment-index]] $k$. The matching [[payment-time]] is $t_k$,
measured from [[valuation-time]] $0$.

Write the level [[coupon-payment]] as $C$ and the promised
[[bond-cash-flow]] on date $k$ as $CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}$.

The number of payment dates is the payment frequency multiplied by the
maturity:

$$
n=m_{\mathrm B}T.
$$

The bond payment frequency is a different concept from the
[[compounding-frequency]] $m$ of the earlier lessons. So it has a different
symbol, with the subscript $\mathrm B$.

## Calculate the coupon

The level coupon payment is the annual coupon rate multiplied by the face
value, divided by the payment frequency:

$$
C=\frac{cF}{m_{\mathrm B}}.
$$

The coupon rate is applied to the face value, not to the market price of the
bond.

## Construct every promised payment

For the bondholder, the promised bond cash flow on date $k$ is the coupon,
plus the face value on the final date:

$$
CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}
=C+\mathbf{1}_{\{k=n\}}F.
$$

The indicator $\mathbf{1}_{\{k=n\}}$ equals one on the final date and zero on
every other date. So the face value is paid only on the final date.

On the regular schedule, the payment time of date $k$, measured from valuation
time, is:

$$
t_k=\frac{k}{m_{\mathrm B}},
\qquad 0\text{ is the valuation time}.
$$

## Model boundary and review note

This lesson describes promised cash flows only. It does not assign a price,
yield, payment probability, or expected recovery.

The contract terms and the level-coupon schedule follow Tuckman &
Serrat[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §1.1, "Government Coupon Bonds": a bond is set by coupon rate, maturity date, and face (par, principal); each semiannual coupon is half the annual rate times face, with principal repaid at maturity (Table 1.1)]. the printed locators.
