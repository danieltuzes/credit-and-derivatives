## Contract first

The long bond forward is obligated to pay the [[forward-delivery-price]]
and receive the named bond at [[forward-delivery-time]]. It does not own the
bond before delivery. Coupons paid before delivery therefore belong to the
current holder.

## Cash-and-carry price

At [[valuation-time]], start with the current [[dirty-bond-price]]
$P^{\mathrm{dirty}}$. Subtract the local
[[predelivery-coupon-present-value]] $I_0^{\mathrm{pre}}$. The remainder is
the prepaid forward value of receiving the bond at delivery. Converting that
prepaid amount into delivery-date currency gives the fair
[[bond-forward-price]]:

$$
\explain{bond-forward-price}{F^B_{0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}}}}
=\frac{
\explain{dirty-bond-price}{P^{\mathrm{dirty}}}
-\explain{predelivery-coupon-present-value}{I_0^{\mathrm{pre}}}
}{
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}})}
}.
$$

For dirty spot USD 960, pre-delivery coupon PV USD 95.45, and delivery factor
0.92, the fair dirty delivery price is USD 939.72826087.

An already-existing contract need not have that delivery price. Its value is
handled by the earlier forward-value lesson; here the output is the fair price
for a new contract.

## Basis consistency

A clean spot quote must first be converted to dirty cash price. Likewise, a
clean delivery quote or strike needs the delivery-date accrued amount. Mixing
clean and dirty values can look like a pricing discrepancy while being only a
unit/convention mismatch.
