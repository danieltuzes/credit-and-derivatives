## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- interpret present value from the same perspective as its cash flows;
- discount each dated deterministic amount;
- add discounted amounts without mixing payment dates.

## Make cash flows comparable before adding

Use the local [[present-value-payment-index]] $k$ to select one row and
the local [[number-of-future-payments]] $n$ to count the included future
rows. At row $k$, write the [[signed-cash-flow]] as $CF_k$, its
[[payment-time]] as $t_k$, and the matching [[discount-factor]] as
$D(0,t_k)$. Write the schedule's [[present-value]] at
[[valuation-time]] $0$ as $PV_0$.

The present value of the deterministic schedule is the sum of the discounted
cash flows:

$$
PV_0=\sum_{k=1}^{n}CF_kD(0,t_k).
\label{eq:present-value-sum}
$$

The index $k$ and the count $n$ are defined only in this lesson. The other
symbols are shared across the course.

Two schedules are compatible when they use the same valuation time,
perspective, currency, and discount factors. Let
$\mathrm{A}\mathbin{\|}\mathrm{B}$ be the schedule that contains every row of
both schedules $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{B}$. For two compatible schedules, the
present value is additive:

$$
PV_0(\mathrm{A}\mathbin{\|}\mathrm{B})=PV_0(\mathrm{A})+PV_0(\mathrm{B}).
\label{eq:present-value-additivity}
$$

<CompactExamples summary="Worked examples (3)">
  <CompactExample label="One payment">

A deterministic receipt of USD 1,000 is paid after two years, and
$D(0,2)=0.9070294785$. The present value of the receipt is:

$$
PV_0
=1000\times0.9070294785
=\text{USD }907.0294785
\approx\text{USD }907.03.
$$

The present value is in USD at [[valuation-time]], from the holder's
perspective.

  </CompactExample>
  <CompactExample label="Mixed signed cash flows">

Suppose the holder receives USD 100 at $t_1$, pays USD 40 at $t_2$, and
receives USD 300 at $t_3$. The discount factors are 0.97, 0.93, and
0.88.

| Payment index $k$ | Cash flow $CF_k$, USD | Discount factor $D(0,t_k)$ | Discounted cash flow $CF_kD(0,t_k)$, USD |
| ----------------: | --------------------: | -------------------------: | ---------------------------------------: |
|                 1 |                  +100 |                       0.97 |                                   +97.00 |
|                 2 |                   -40 |                       0.93 |                                   -37.20 |
|                 3 |                  +300 |                       0.88 |                                  +264.00 |

The present value is the sum of the discounted cash flows:

$$
PV_0=97.00-37.20+264.00=\text{USD }323.80.
$$

Discounting does not change the sign of a cash flow. Reversing the perspective
would change the sign of the present value.

  </CompactExample>
  <CompactExample label="Additivity">

Combining two schedules keeps every row, including duplicate payments. For
compatible schedules, a property test of the domain code checks
[[eq-present-value-additivity]]. The compatibility conditions are necessary:
present values in different currencies, or at different valuation dates,
cannot be added without another model.

  </CompactExample>
</CompactExamples>

## Model boundary and review note

This lesson covers deterministic present value only. It omits curves, calendars, uncertainty,
credit, recovery, liquidity, tax, and funding.

The rule [[eq-present-value-sum]] follows Tuckman &
Serrat[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §1.2, price as the sum of each cash flow times its discount factor (eqs. 1.1-1.3); full price equals present value (eq. 1.5)]; Hull states the same
principle[@hull-options-futures; Ch. 4, "Bond Pricing"]. The lesson
stays `draft` pending human confirmation of the printed locators.
