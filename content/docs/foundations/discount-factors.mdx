## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- explain the units and meaning of a discount factor;
- calculate one from a stated nominal rate, frequency, and time;
- distinguish discounting from compounding and from payment probability.

## Undo accumulation

On this page, $t$ is the shorter generic-horizon form of the shared
[[payment-time]], measured in years from [[valuation-time]] $0$.
Write the [[compounding-frequency]] as $m$, the
[[periodic-rate]] as $r_m$, the [[accumulation-factor]] as
$A(0,t)$, and the [[discount-factor]] as $D(0,t)$.

The accumulation factor grows a time-zero unit forward. The discount factor
reverses that operation:

$$
D(0,t)=\frac{1}{A(0,t)}=\left(1+r_m\right)^{-mt}.
\label{eq:discount-factor-def}
$$

The factor and each ingredient retain separate semantic meanings even when
they appear in one expression.

## Meaning before arithmetic

If $D(0,2)=0.94$, then one deterministic unit paid at $t=2$ years has value
0.94 units at valuation time. Write this local
[[discounted-unit-value]] as $V_0(1_t)$. At the stated two-year horizon,

$$
V_0(1_2)
=1\times D(0,2)
=0.94.
$$

The number 0.94 is not a 94% annual interest rate and is not a 94% probability
that payment occurs.

<CompactExamples summary="Worked examples (3)">
  <CompactExample label="Annual compounding">

At a 5% [[nominal-annual-rate]] with $m=1$ and $t=2$,

$$
D(0,2)
=\left(1+\frac{0.05}{1}\right)^{-1\times2}
=\frac{1}{1.05^2}
\approx0.907029.
$$

  </CompactExample>
  <CompactExample label="Semiannual compounding">

At a 6% nominal annual rate compounded twice per year for 1.5 years,

$$
D(0,1.5)
=\left(1+\frac{0.06}{2}\right)^{-2(1.5)}
=\frac{1}{1.03^3}
\approx0.915142.
$$

The time horizon contains three compounding periods; using exponent 1.5 would
silently mix years with half-year periods.

  </CompactExample>
  <CompactExample label="Read a small curve">

Under the same 6% nominal semiannual model:

| Time $t$, years | Periods $mt$ | Discount factor $D(0,t)$ |
| --------------: | -----------: | -----------------------: |
|             0.0 |            0 |                 1.000000 |
|             0.5 |            1 |                 0.970874 |
|             1.0 |            2 |                 0.942596 |
|             1.5 |            3 |                 0.915142 |

With the stated non-negative rate, later deterministic units receive
non-increasing discount factors. They are strictly smaller when the rate is
strictly positive.

  </CompactExample>
</CompactExamples>

## Model boundary and review note

The rate is flat across time and cash flows are deterministic. The page does
not yet teach spot curves, interpolation, day counts, default, recovery,
liquidity, or funding.

The discount-factor definition [[eq-discount-factor-def]] follows
Tuckman & Serrat[@tuckman-serrat-fixed-income; §1.2, the discount factor as the value today of one unit paid at t; §2.4, eqs. 2.17-2.19, the semiannually compounded form]; Hull gives the same
present-value-of-a-future-unit
reading[@hull-options-futures; Ch. 4, "Bond Pricing"]. The lesson
stays `draft` pending human confirmation of the printed locators.
