## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the holder, writer, underlying, strike, and expiry of a European option;
- calculate long and short call and put payoffs;
- distinguish current value or premium, expiry payoff, and profit.

## Rights and obligations

A European call gives its holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy one
unit of the underlying for the \term\{option-strike-price\} $K$ at the
\term\{option-expiry-time\} $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$. A European put gives the holder
the corresponding right to sell. The writer must perform if the holder
exercises. Hull introduces these call/put and European-exercise distinctions
\cite\{hull-options-futures\}\{Ch. 1 §1.5, printed pp. 7-9, call and put option
rights and European exercise\}.

## Expiry payoff

Let the \term\{derivative-underlying-value\} at expiry be
$S_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}$. The local \term\{long-call-expiry-payoff\} and
\term\{long-put-expiry-payoff\}, per unit, are

$$
\Pi_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{call}}
=\max\!\left(S_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}-K,0\right),
$$

$$
\Pi_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{put}}
=\max\!\left(K-S_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}},0\right).
$$

The maximum with zero is the holder's right to decline an unfavorable
exercise. A writer's signed payoff is the negative of the matching holder
payoff.

For $S_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}=115$ and $K=100$, one call pays 15 and one put pays
zero. For $S_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}=70$ and $K=85$, one put pays 15; a writer of
three such puts has signed payoff $-45$.

## Value, premium, payoff, and profit are different

The \term\{call-option-value\} $c_t$ or \term\{put-option-value\} $p_t$ is a
current value. At inception, the amount actually paid by the holder is the
local \term\{option-premium-paid\} $\pi_0$ under the stated settlement
convention. The payoff is determined later from the expiry state.

If financing is deliberately ignored, local \term\{option-holder-expiry-profit\}
for a call is

$$
G_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}
=\Pi_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{call}}-\pi_0.
$$

Thus a call purchased for 6 that later pays 9 has payoff 9 and profit 3 under
that shortcut. A fully dated profit calculation would carry the premium and
any other cash flows to one common comparison date. Hull's option examples
distinguish premium, exercise, payoff, and profit
\cite\{hull-options-futures\}\{Ch. 9 practice questions 9.1-9.2 and 9.9-9.10,
printed pp. 211-212, payoff versus profit\}.

## Model boundary and review note

This lesson covers European calls and puts only. It does not yet value them or
cover American or Bermudan exercise, automatic exercise, assignment, settlement
delay, corporate actions, collateral, counterparty default, or barrier and other
path-dependent terms.

The payoff functions live in a pure tested domain module. Sources, notation,
examples, code, and answer keys remain `draft` pending independent human review.
