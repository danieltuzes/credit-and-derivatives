## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- construct the two-position portfolio that matches both state payoffs;
- calculate the tree's risk-neutral up-state weight;
- show that replication and discounted risk-neutral expectation give the same value.

## Two states, two traded positions

Let the underlying be worth $\explain{derivative-underlying-value}{S_0}$ at
\term\{valuation-time\} and take exactly the local values
\term\{binomial-up-underlying-value\}
$\explain{binomial-up-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{up}}}$ or
\term\{binomial-down-underlying-value\}
$\explain{binomial-down-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{down}}}$ at the
\term\{option-expiry-time\} $T_{\mathrm{opt}}$. A claim pays local
\term\{binomial-up-claim-payoff\}
$\explain{binomial-up-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{up}}}$ or
\term\{binomial-down-claim-payoff\}
$\explain{binomial-down-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{down}}}$.

The local \term\{binomial-hedge-units\} that matches the payoff difference are

$$
\explain{binomial-hedge-units}{\Delta}
=\frac{\explain{binomial-up-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{up}}}
-\explain{binomial-down-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{down}}}}
{\explain{binomial-up-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{up}}}
-\explain{binomial-down-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{down}}}}.
$$

The local \term\{binomial-risk-free-expiry-cash\} then matches the down state:

$$
\explain{binomial-risk-free-expiry-cash}{M_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}}
=\explain{binomial-down-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{down}}}
-\explain{binomial-hedge-units}{\Delta}\,
\explain{binomial-down-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{down}}}.
$$

Consequently the local \term\{binomial-claim-value\} of the replicating
portfolio is

$$
\explain{binomial-claim-value}{V_0}
=\explain{binomial-hedge-units}{\Delta}\,
\explain{derivative-underlying-value}{S_0}
+\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}})}
\explain{binomial-risk-free-expiry-cash}{M_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}}.
$$

## The same value with risk-neutral weights

The no-arbitrage tree chooses local \term\{binomial-risk-neutral-up-weight\}
$q$ so the underlying itself is priced correctly:

$$
\explain{binomial-risk-neutral-up-weight}{q}
=\frac{\explain{derivative-underlying-value}{S_0}/D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}})
-\explain{binomial-down-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{down}}}}
{\explain{binomial-up-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{up}}}
-\explain{binomial-down-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{down}}}}.
$$

Using the \term\{risk-neutral-probability-measure\} $\mathbb{Q}$, the same claim
value is

$$
\explain{binomial-claim-value}{V_0}
=\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}})}
\left(\explain{binomial-risk-neutral-up-weight}{q}\,
\explain{binomial-up-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{up}}}
+(1-\explain{binomial-risk-neutral-up-weight}{q})
\explain{binomial-down-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{down}}}\right).
$$

Hull derives the one-step hedge and then shows that its no-arbitrage value equals
the discounted risk-neutral expected payoff
\cite\{hull-options-futures\}\{Ch. 12 §§12.1-12.2, printed pp. 253-259,
one-step binomial replication and risk-neutral valuation\}.

## Worked call example

Let $\explain{derivative-underlying-value}{S_0}=100$,
$\explain{binomial-up-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{up}}}=120$,
$\explain{binomial-down-underlying-value}{S^{\mathrm{down}}}=80$,
$\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,T_{\mathrm{opt}})}=1/1.05$, and let the
\term\{option-strike-price\} be
$\explain{option-strike-price}{K}=100$. The call payoffs are
$\explain{binomial-up-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{up}}}=20$ and
$\explain{binomial-down-claim-payoff}{X^{\mathrm{down}}}=0$.

Then $\explain{binomial-hedge-units}{\Delta}=0.5$,
$\explain{binomial-risk-free-expiry-cash}{M_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}}=-40$, and
$\explain{binomial-risk-neutral-up-weight}{q}=0.625$. Both methods give

$$
\explain{binomial-claim-value}{V_0}
=0.5(100)+\frac{-40}{1.05}
=\frac{0.625(20)+0.375(0)}{1.05}
=11.9047619.
$$

The negative cash position is borrowing; it is not a negative option value.

## From one period to a path

Repeating this valuation one step at a time produces backward induction on a
multi-period tree. Later bond-option lessons need node-specific discounting and
an additional survival/default state. A default-triggered knock-out cannot be
valued from a terminal distribution alone because the option may terminate
before expiry.

## Model boundary and review note

This complete two-state model has one non-income-paying traded underlying, one
risk-free account, and deterministic discounting. It does not estimate
real-world probabilities, volatility, multiple time steps, stochastic rates,
default, or transaction and funding effects.

The two valuation methods are implemented together in a pure tested domain
module. The hand-calculated answer and implementation both remain subject to
independent human quantitative review.
