## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- read the long and short obligations of a forward;
- distinguish delivery price, current forward price, and contract value;
- calculate a fair delivery price with deterministic income;
- calculate the signed value of an existing forward.

## An obligation, not an option

The \term\{forward-delivery-time\} $T_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ is when the exchange
occurs. Under the physical-settlement convention, the long pays the
\term\{forward-delivery-price\} $K_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ and receives one unit of
the asset. If its \term\{derivative-underlying-value\} then is
$S_{T_{\mathrm{fwd}}}$, the local \term\{long-forward-expiry-payoff\} per unit
is

$$
\Pi_{T_{\mathrm{fwd}}}^{\mathrm{long}}
=S_{T_{\mathrm{fwd}}}-K_{\mathrm{fwd}}.
$$

The short has the exact opposite payoff. Hull distinguishes this bilateral
obligation from an option holder's exercise right and gives the corresponding
long and short payoff directions\cite\{hull-options-futures\}\{Ch. 1 §1.3,
printed pp. 5-7, forward contracts and payoffs\}.

## Fair delivery price at inception

The \term\{forward-price\} $F_{0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}}}$ is the delivery price that
would make a new contract worth zero at \term\{valuation-time\} $0$. Suppose
the underlying is worth $S_0$ and the local
\term\{deterministic-income-present-value\} paid before delivery is $I_0$.
The forward long does not own the asset before delivery, so the prepaid value of
what will be delivered is $S_0-I_0$.

Under the stated cash-and-carry assumptions, with the delivery-time
\term\{discount-factor\} $D(0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}})$, the law of one price gives

$$
F_{0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}}}
=\frac{S_0-I_0}{D(0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}})}.
$$

Hull derives the no-income and known-income forward prices by matching a spot
purchase and financing strategy to the forward exchange
\cite\{hull-options-futures\}\{Ch. 5 §§5.3-5.5, printed pp. 103-108, cash-and-carry
forward pricing and known income\}.

For $S_0=100$, $I_0=4$, and $D(0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}})=0.95$,

$$
F_{0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}}}=\frac{100-4}{0.95}=101.0526316.
$$

The nonzero delivery price is not an inception premium. The fair new contract's
signed value is zero because its fixed delivery price equals the current forward
price.

## An existing forward can gain or lose value

After inception, $K_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ remains fixed while the current forward
price can change. For local \term\{forward-contract-quantity\} $N$, the
\term\{forward-contract-value\} to the long at time zero is

$$
V_0^{\mathrm{fwd}}
=N D(0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}})
\left(F_{0,T_{\mathrm{fwd}}}-K_{\mathrm{fwd}}\right).
$$

If $N=10$, the current forward price is 98, the contract delivery price is 102,
and the discount factor is 0.94, then

$$
V_0^{\mathrm{fwd}}=10(0.94)(98-102)=-37.60.
$$

The short's value is USD +37.60 under the same inputs. Hull derives this
discounted forward-price difference for an existing contract
\cite\{hull-options-futures\}\{Ch. 5 §5.7, printed pp. 109-111, valuation of
existing forward contracts\}.

## Model boundary and review note

This is a teaching cash-and-carry model for an investment asset with known
income and deterministic discounting. It does not model futures-style daily
settlement, repo specialness, collateral, counterparty credit, taxes, borrowing
asymmetry, delivery options, or an asset that cannot be financed or shorted on
the assumed terms.

The formulas are implemented in a pure tested domain module. Sources, notation,
examples, code, and answer keys remain `draft` pending independent human review.
