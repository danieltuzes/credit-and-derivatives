## The standalone contract

A European bond call gives the holder the right, but not the obligation, to
buy a named bond at \term\{option-expiry-time\}. A put gives the right to sell.
This is not the issuer call feature inside a callable bond; the party holding
the right and the surrounding instrument are different.

## Align cash-price units before payoff

The local \term\{bond-option-expiry-value\}
$B_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{dirty}}$ and \term\{option-strike-price\} $K$
must use the same dirty cash basis. If a contract states a clean strike, add
exercise-date \term\{accrued-interest\} before comparison.

For the lesson's exercise immediately after any expiry-date coupon, the call
and put payoffs are

$$
\explain{bond-call-expiry-payoff}{X^{B,\mathrm{call}}_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}}
=\max\left(
\explain{bond-option-expiry-value}{B_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{dirty}}}
-\explain{option-strike-price}{K},0\right),
$$

$$
\explain{bond-put-expiry-payoff}{X^{B,\mathrm{put}}_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}}
=\max\left(
\explain{option-strike-price}{K}
-\explain{bond-option-expiry-value}{B_{T_{\mathrm{opt}}}^{\mathrm{dirty}}},0\right).
$$

Backward induction then turns those payoffs into a
\term\{lattice-node-value\}. The lattice must first produce bond values at option
expiry; payoff arithmetic alone is not a bond option model.

## Coupon and exercise ordering

An expiry-date coupon is paid before exercise in this slice, so the expiry
bond value is ex-coupon. A contract exercising before the coupon or transferring
coupon entitlement would require different terminal inputs.

quoted/clean strikes follow Hull, Chapter 28 §28.1 \cite\{hull-options-futures\}.

subsequent issuer-default state can remain explicit.
