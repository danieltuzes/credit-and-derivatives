## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- interpret recovery as a fraction of a named base paid at a named time;
- calculate loss given default as the complement of recovery;
- calculate present value in one explicitly labeled
  recovery-of-par-paid-at-maturity model.

## Name the recovery convention before calculating

This lesson uses one narrow convention only. A claim has positive local
\term\{recovery-par-amount\} $F$ and one local
\term\{recovery-payment-time\} $T$, measured from
\term\{valuation-time\} $0$.

- If the reference entity survives through $T$, the holder receives $F$ at
  $T$.
- If it defaults at any earlier time, the holder receives $RF$ at $T$, where
  \term\{recovery-rate\} $R$ is deterministic.

Both state payments occur at the same scheduled time. That timing is a model
assumption, not an assertion about an actual instrument. In particular, this
page does not teach recovery paid at default, recovery of market value,
settlement delay, or a CDS settlement mechanism.

Tuckman and Serrat define a bond recovery rate relative to face amount and
identify the complementary loss fraction in their credit-loss discussion
\cite\{tuckman-serrat-fixed-income\}\{Ch. 14 §14.2, printed p. 353\}. The
payment-at-maturity timing on this page is an explicit teaching convention,
not a claim that this is the only recovery convention.

## Recovery and loss given default are complements here

The \term\{loss-given-default\} fraction is

$$
\mathrm{LGD}=1-R,
\qquad 0\leq R\leq1.
$$

For example, $R=0.40$ implies $\mathrm{LGD}=0.60$ in this synthetic model. The
number is chosen for arithmetic only; the lesson makes no claim that 40% is a
market convention, forecast, or appropriate assumption for any real entity.

## Take the state-weighted maturity amount, then discount it

The supplied \term\{survival-probability\} is
$\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,T)}$ under the
\term\{risk-neutral-probability-measure\} $\mathbb{Q}$. Under the one-default
model, the pricing probability assigned to default before $T$ is
$1-S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,T)$.

For this single interval from $0$ through $T$, $1-S(0,T)$ is the
\term\{interval-default-probability\}. The state-weighted amount paid at $T$
is

$$
F\left[S(0,T)+R\left(1-S(0,T)\right)\right].
$$

This is the two-event specialization of the expectation-by-partition rule:
survival pays $F$ and default pays $RF$. Shreve gives the finite-valued
expectation rule\cite\{shreve-stochastic-calculus-finance-ii\}\{Ch. 1 §1.3,
printed pp. 13–18\}; Tuckman and Serrat apply survival/default event weighting
and discounting to credit cash flows\cite\{tuckman-serrat-fixed-income\}\{Ch.
14 §14.7, printed pp. 371–372, eq. 14.8 and Table 14.11\}.

Both state amounts are paid at $T$, so the same supplied
\term\{discount-factor\} $\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,T)}$ applies. The
\term\{recovery-of-par-present-value\} is

$$
\explain{recovery-of-par-present-value}{PV_0^{\mathrm{RoP}}}
=F\,\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,T)}
\left[
\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,T)}
+R\left(1-\explain{survival-probability}{S^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,T)}\right)
\right].
$$

This is a \term\{present-value\} calculation from the asset-holder perspective:
the modeled future receipts and their value are positive. A purchase price paid
by that holder would have the opposite cash-flow sign.

  summary="Worked examples"

>
  <CompactExample label="Partial recovery">

Let $F=\text{USD }1{,}000$, $D(0,T)=0.96$, $S(0,T)=0.92$, and $R=0.40$. The
state-weighted fraction of par paid at maturity is

$$
0.92+0.40(1-0.92)=0.952.
$$

Therefore,

$$
PV_0^{\mathrm{RoP}}
=1000(0.96)(0.952)
=\text{USD }913.92.
$$

The undiscounted expected maturity amount is USD 952.00; discounting then
converts it to valuation time.

  </CompactExample>

  <CompactExample label="Zero-recovery boundary">

Let $F=\text{USD }1{,}000$, $D(0,T)=0.94$, $S(0,T)=0.90$, and $R=0$. The
default state contributes no payment:

$$
PV_0^{\mathrm{RoP}}
=1000(0.94)\left[0.90+0(0.10)\right]
=\text{USD }846.00.
$$

This boundary is the discounted survival-state payment, not a statement about
real recovery outcomes.

  </CompactExample>

  <CompactExample label="Full-recovery boundary">

Keep $F=\text{USD }1{,}000$, $D(0,T)=0.94$, and $S(0,T)=0.90$, but set $R=1$.
Both states now pay full par at the same time:

$$
PV_0^{\mathrm{RoP}}
=1000(0.94)\left[0.90+1(0.10)\right]
=\text{USD }940.00.
$$

Within this narrow timing convention, the survival probability drops out at
full recovery because both modeled states have the same amount and date.

  </CompactExample>
</CompactExamples>

## Model boundary and review note

The model has one maturity date, one deterministic recovery fraction, one
supplied survival probability, and one supplied discount factor. Recovery is a
fraction of par paid at maturity even when default occurs earlier. The lesson
omits default-time settlement, coupons, multiple intervals, stochastic
recovery, market-value recovery, interest/default dependence, calibration,
liquidity, funding, taxes, and counterparty risk.

All materially changed content and source locators remain \`draft\` pending
