## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- interpret yield to maturity as a model-dependent single-rate summary;
- state its annualization and compounding convention;
- calculate price from a supplied yield without changing promised cash flows.

## What yield means in this toy model

\term\{yield-to-maturity\} is the one nominal annual rate, compounded at the
\term\{bond-payment-frequency\}, that makes the discounted promised payments
equal the \term\{bond-price\}:

$$
\explain{bond-price}{P_0}
=
\sum_{\explain{yield-pricing-index}{k}=1}^{n}
\frac{
  \explain{bond-cash-flow}{
    CF_{\explain{yield-pricing-index}{k}}^{\mathrm{bond}}
  }
}{
  \left(
    1+
    \frac{
      \explain{yield-to-maturity}{y^{(m_B)}}
    }{
      \explain{bond-payment-frequency}{m_B}
    }
  \right)^{
    \explain{yield-pricing-index}{k}
  }
}.
$$

The semantic key matters because the glyph $y$ could otherwise mean a spot
rate, effective annual rate, expected return, or another quote. Here it means
only the explicitly defined bond yield.

## Coupon rate and yield do different jobs

- The coupon rate fixes the promised \term\{coupon-payment\} in the contract.
- The supplied yield controls the denominators used by this pricing summary.

Changing the yield input does not rewrite the bond's coupons or
\term\{face-value\}.

## Example 1: annual payments

A two-year, 5% annual-coupon bond with USD 1,000 face value pays USD 50 and USD
1,050. At a 6% annual yield with annual compounding,

$$
P_0
=\frac{50}{1.06}
+\frac{1050}{1.06^2}
\approx\text{USD }981.67.
$$

This migrates the original skeleton's numerical example without changing its
expected value.

## Example 2: semiannual payments and compounding

For the same face value and coupon rate over two years, but with semiannual
payments, $C=25$, $m_B=2$, and $n=4$. A 6% nominal annual yield gives a
3% periodic rate:

$$
P_0
=\frac{25}{1.03}
+\frac{25}{1.03^2}
+\frac{25}{1.03^3}
+\frac{1025}{1.03^4}
\approx\text{USD }981.41.
$$

The annual and semiannual examples use the same percentage number but not the
same timing or compounding model.

## Example 3: zero-coupon special case

With no coupons,

$$
P_0=\frac{F}{(1+y^{(m_B)}/m_B)^{m_BT}}.
$$

For USD 1,000 due in three years at 4% with annual compounding, the price is
approximately USD 889.00.

## Model boundary and review note

This page prices from a supplied yield; it does not solve numerically for yield
from a price. A real bond implementation also needs schedules, day counts,
accrued interest, clean/dirty price, and potentially credit and option models.
