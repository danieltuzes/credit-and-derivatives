## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to calculate a toy bond's price from its
promised cash-flow schedule and supplied discount factors.

## Bond pricing is a present-value application

Use the \term\{bond-payment-index\} $k$ to select one of the
\term\{number-of-bond-payments\} $n$. Write the promised
\term\{bond-cash-flow\} as $CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}$, its
\term\{payment-time\} as $t_k$, and its matching \term\{discount-factor\} as
$D(0,t_k)$. Write the \term\{bond-price\}, which is the
\term\{present-value\} at \term\{valuation-time\} $0$, as $P_0$.

The pricing rule is

$$
P_0=\sum_{k=1}^{n}CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}D(0,t_k).
$$

The semantic key for price is not overloaded with clean price, quoted price,
or a probability. Those concepts are outside this slice.

  <CompactExample label="Two supplied factors">

The annual-coupon bond from the preceding lesson pays USD 50 after one year and
USD 1,050 after two years. Suppose the matching factors are 0.96 and 0.91.

| Payment index $k$ | Cash flow, USD | Discount factor | Present value, USD |
| ----------------: | -------------: | --------------: | -----------------: |
|                 1 |             50 |            0.96 |              48.00 |
|                 2 |          1,050 |            0.91 |             955.50 |

Therefore,

$$
P_0=50(0.96)+1050(0.91)=\text{USD }1{,}003.50.
$$

The price is above USD 1,000 because this particular pair of supplied factors
makes the promised payments worth more than face value. No one-number yield has
been introduced yet.

  </CompactExample>

  <CompactExample label="Unfamiliar schedule">

Consider payments of USD 20 at 0.5 years, USD 20 at 1.0 year, and USD 1,020 at
1.5 years with discount factors 0.98, 0.95, and 0.92:

$$
P_0
=20(0.98)+20(0.95)+1020(0.92)
=19.60+19.00+938.40
=\text{USD }977.00.
$$

  </CompactExample>

  <CompactExample label="Zero-coupon boundary">

If every coupon is zero, only the \term\{face-value\} $F$ at
\term\{maturity-time\} $T$ remains:

$$
P_0=F D(0,T).
$$

This is the same pricing rule, not a separate exception.

  </CompactExample>

## Error pattern: discount after adding

Adding payments from different dates first and then multiplying the total by
one unnamed discount factor is generally incomplete. Each payment needs the
factor for its own time. A single common factor is valid only when the model
genuinely assigns that same factor to every included amount.

## Model boundary and review note

Discount factors are supplied, not calibrated. Credit risk is absent even
though the broader project concerns credit products.

The rule $P_0=\sum_k CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}\,D(0,t_k)$ follows Tuckman &
Serrat\cite\{tuckman-serrat-fixed-income\}\{§1.2, each bond price as its cash
flows times the matching discount factors (eqs. 1.1-1.3), checked against market
prices in Table 1.4\}; Hull discounts each cash flow at its own zero rate the
same way\cite\{hull-options-futures\}\{Ch. 4, "Bond Pricing"\}. The lesson
stays `draft` pending human confirmation of the printed locators.
