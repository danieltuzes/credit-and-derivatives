## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to calculate a toy bond's price from its
promised cash-flow schedule and supplied discount factors.

## Bond pricing is a present-value application

The \term\{bond-price\} is the \term\{present-value\} at
\term\{valuation-time\}:

$$
\explain{bond-price}{P_0}
=
\sum_{\explain{bond-price-payment-index}{k}=1}^{n}
\explain{bond-cash-flow}{
  CF_{\explain{bond-price-payment-index}{k}}^{\mathrm{bond}}
}
\explain{discount-factor}{
  D(0,\explain{payment-time}{t_k})
}.
$$

The semantic key for price is not overloaded with clean price, quoted price,
or a probability. Those concepts are outside this slice.

## Example 1: two supplied factors

The annual-coupon bond from the preceding lesson pays USD 50 after one year and
USD 1,050 after two years. Suppose the matching factors are 0.96 and 0.91.

| Payment index $k$ | Cash flow, USD | Discount factor | Present value, USD |
| ----------------: | -------------: | --------------: | -----------------: |
|                 1 |             50 |            0.96 |              48.00 |
|                 2 |          1,050 |            0.91 |             955.50 |

Therefore,

$$
P_0=50(0.96)+1050(0.91)=\text{USD }1{,}003.50.
$$

The price is above USD 1,000 because this particular pair of supplied factors
makes the promised payments worth more than face value. No one-number yield has
been introduced yet.

## Example 2: unfamiliar schedule

Consider payments of USD 20 at 0.5 years, USD 20 at 1.0 year, and USD 1,020 at
1.5 years with discount factors 0.98, 0.95, and 0.92:

$$
P_0
=20(0.98)+20(0.95)+1020(0.92)
=19.60+19.00+938.40
=\text{USD }977.00.
$$

## Example 3: a zero-coupon boundary

If every coupon is zero, only face value at maturity remains:

$$
P_0=F D(0,T).
$$

This is the same pricing rule, not a separate exception.

## Error pattern: discount after adding

The expression

$$
\left(\sum_k CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}\right)D
$$

is generally incomplete because payments at different times require different
factors. It is valid only if the same factor genuinely applies to every
included amount.
