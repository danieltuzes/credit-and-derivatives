## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- identify the terms that determine a toy fixed-rate bond's payments;
- calculate the level coupon amount;
- construct the complete promised cash-flow schedule.

## Read the contract before valuing it

The simplified contract specifies \term\{face-value\}
$\explain{face-value}{F}$, \term\{annual-coupon-rate\}
$\explain{annual-coupon-rate}{c}$, \term\{bond-payment-frequency\}
$\explain{bond-payment-frequency}{m_B}$, and \term\{maturity-time\}
$\explain{maturity-time}{T}$.

The local number of dates is

$$
\explain{number-of-bond-payments}{n}
=
\explain{bond-payment-frequency}{m_B}
\explain{maturity-time}{T}.
$$

The page uses $m_B$, not the generic rate symbol $m$, so payment frequency
and compounding frequency remain distinct concepts.

## Calculate the coupon

The level \term\{coupon-payment\} is

$$
\explain{coupon-payment}{C}
=
\frac{
  \explain{annual-coupon-rate}{c}
  \explain{face-value}{F}
}{
  \explain{bond-payment-frequency}{m_B}
}.
$$

The coupon rate is applied to face value, not the bond's eventual market price.

## Construct every promised payment

For the bondholder, the promised \term\{bond-cash-flow\} is

$$
\explain{bond-cash-flow}{
  CF_{\explain{bond-payment-index}{k}}^{\mathrm{bond}}
}
=
\explain{coupon-payment}{C}
+
\mathbf{1}_{\{
  \explain{bond-payment-index}{k}
  =
  \explain{number-of-bond-payments}{n}
\}}
\explain{face-value}{F}.
$$

The indicator contributes face value only on the final date.

For the regular schedule, each date is measured from
\term\{valuation-time\}:

$$
\explain{payment-time}{t_k}
=
\frac{\explain{bond-payment-index}{k}}
{\explain{bond-payment-frequency}{m_B}},
\qquad
\explain{valuation-time}{0}\text{ is the valuation time}.
$$

## Example 1: annual coupons

A two-year bond has USD 1,000 face value, a 5% annual coupon rate, and one
payment per year:

$$
C=\frac{0.05\times1000}{1}=\text{USD }50.
$$

| Payment index $k$ | Payment time $t_k$, years | Coupon, USD | Principal, USD | Total, USD |
| ----------------: | ------------------------: | ----------: | -------------: | ---------: |
|                 1 |                       1.0 |          50 |              0 |         50 |
|                 2 |                       2.0 |          50 |          1,000 |      1,050 |

## Example 2: semiannual coupons

An 18-month bond has USD 1,000 face value, a 4% annual coupon rate, and two
payments per year:

$$
C=\frac{0.04\times1000}{2}=\text{USD }20,
\qquad
n=2\times1.5=3.
$$

Its payments at 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 years are USD 20, USD 20, and USD 1,020.

## Example 3: quarterly coupon amount

For USD 500 face value, a 4.8% annual coupon rate, and quarterly payments,

$$
C=\frac{0.048\times500}{4}=\text{USD }6.
$$

Maturity determines how many such payments occur; it does not change the
periodic coupon amount under the level-coupon assumption.
