## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- explain why a fixed positive-cash-flow bond's price falls when yield rises;
- hold contract cash flows fixed while changing only the discounting input;
- recognize that the price-yield relationship is curved rather than linear.

## Direction comes from every denominator

For promised positive \term\{bond-cash-flow\} amounts,

$$
\explain{price-yield-curve}{P(y)}
=
\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{CF_k^{\mathrm{bond}}}
{\left(1+\explain{yield-to-maturity}{y}/m_B\right)^k}.
$$

Within the stated non-negative-yield domain, if yield rises while every cash
flow stays fixed, every positive denominator increases. Every discounted
contribution decreases, so \term\{bond-price\} decreases. The shock table also
assumes the new yield remains in that domain.

| Yield shock                         | Denominators | Price change                         |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| $\explain{yield-shock}{\Delta y}>0$ | Increase     | $\explain{price-change}{\Delta P}<0$ |
| $\explain{yield-shock}{\Delta y}<0$ | Decrease     | $\explain{price-change}{\Delta P}>0$ |

This statement is conditional on the promised cash flows being held fixed.

## Example 1: three points are already a curve

For a five-year bond with a 5% annual coupon rate, semiannual payments, and face
value 100:

| Nominal annual yield | Price per 100 face |
| -------------------: | -----------------: |
|                   5% |           100.0000 |
|                   6% |            95.7349 |
|                   7% |            91.6834 |

The price falls as yield rises. The two one-percentage-point price changes are
not identical, which rules out a straight line.

## Example 2: equal shocks, unequal price moves

Starting from 5% yield:

- a fall to 4% raises price from 100.0000 to 104.4913, a gain of 4.4913;
- a rise to 6% lowers price from 100.0000 to 95.7349, a loss of 4.2651.

The shock sizes are equal, but the absolute price changes differ. This is the
qualitative curvature competency. Duration, DV01, and convexity are not yet
being calculated.

## Example 3: what must remain fixed

If an issuer changes a coupon, a default occurs, or the settlement date moves,
then more than yield has changed. The simple inverse-direction comparison no
longer isolates one input.

## Model boundary and review note

The plotted curve is a toy yield summary, not a credit curve or recommendation.
It omits accrued interest, term-structure effects, default, recovery, liquidity,
tax, funding, and embedded options.
