## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- construct the promised cash flows of a simplified fixed-coupon bond;
- calculate its price from a flat yield;
- explain the direction of the price–yield relationship.

## From contract to cash flows

A two-year, 5% annual-coupon bond with USD 1,000 face value pays USD 50 after
year one and USD 1,050 after year two. Under a flat 6% annual yield,

$$
P(0.06)=\frac{50}{1.06}+\frac{1050}{1.06^2}\approx981.67.
$$

The coupon rate determines promised coupon cash flows. The yield is the rate
used here to summarize their discounting; it is not another payment made by
the issuer.

## Why price and yield move in opposite directions

For positive fixed cash flows, increasing the yield increases every
discounting denominator. Each cash flow contributes less present value, so the
total price falls. The curve is not a straight line; duration and convexity will
formalize its local slope and curvature in the next bond slice.

## Model boundary

Real bond valuation introduces schedules, calendars, accrued interest, clean
and dirty prices, day-count conventions, rate curves, credit and liquidity,
repo or funding, and sometimes embedded options. This lab intentionally
implements none of them yet.
