## The price paid is not always the price quoted

A bond screen commonly shows the 	erm{clean-bond-price}, also called the flat
or quoted price. The buyer pays the 	erm{dirty-bond-price}, also called the
full, cash, or invoice price. The bridge is 	erm{accrued-interest}:

$$
\explain{dirty-bond-price}{P^{\mathrm{dirty}}}
=\explain{clean-bond-price}{P^{\mathrm{clean}}}
+\explain{accrued-interest}{AI}.
$$

On a coupon date, accrued interest is zero and the two prices coincide. That
is why the earlier coupon-date bond model could use one bond-price symbol.

## Actual/actual within one coupon period

Under this lesson's narrow convention, let the local
	erm{coupon-period-elapsed-days} be $n_{\mathrm{elapsed}}$ and the local
	erm{coupon-period-total-days} be $n_{\mathrm{period}}$. For one
	erm{coupon-payment} $C$,

$$
\explain{accrued-interest}{AI}
=\explain{coupon-payment}{C}
\frac{\explain{coupon-period-elapsed-days}{n_{\mathrm{elapsed}}}}
{\explain{coupon-period-total-days}{n_{\mathrm{period}}}}.
$$

For the dated example February 15, 2021 to August 15, 2021, settlement on
May 17 is 91 actual days into a 181-day period. A USD 31.25 coupon therefore
has accrued interest USD $31.25\times91/181=15.71132597$.

The software validates ISO dates and counts UTC calendar days. It does not
pretend to be a production schedule engine: business-day rules, ex-coupon
periods, stubs, and other day counts require separate conventions.

## Why this matters for derivatives

Bond forwards and options settle cash against a price basis. If an exercise
strike is quoted clean while the modeled bond value is dirty, add the
exercise-date accrued amount to the strike before calculating payoff. Coupons
before delivery also have to be assigned to the correct owner.
