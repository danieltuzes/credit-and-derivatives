## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- explain what a discount factor means;
- calculate a discount factor under annual compounding;
- discount and add deterministic cash flows.

## One future dollar is not one dollar today

A discount factor, $D(0,t)$, converts one unit paid at future time $t$ into its
value today. Under a deliberately simple annual-compounding model,

$$
D(0,t)=\frac{1}{(1+y)^t}.
$$

For a future cash flow $CF_t$,

$$
PV(CF_t)=CF_tD(0,t).
$$

The rate $y$ and time $t$ must use compatible units and conventions. A discount
factor is not itself an interest rate, and in this deterministic lesson it is
not a probability of payment.

## Worked example

At 5% for two years,

$$
D(0,2)=\frac{1}{1.05^2}\approx0.907029.
$$

A certain USD 1,000 payment therefore has present value of approximately USD
907.03. For several dated payments, discount each one to today before adding
them.

## Model boundary

This example ignores term structures, day counts, calendars, uncertain cash
flows, default, recovery, liquidity, and funding. Those are additions to the
model, not details to hide inside a default.
