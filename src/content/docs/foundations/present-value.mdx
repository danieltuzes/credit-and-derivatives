## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- interpret present value from the same perspective as its cash flows;
- discount each dated deterministic amount;
- add discounted amounts without mixing payment dates.

## Make values comparable before adding

Use the local \term\{present-value-payment-index\} $k$ to select one row and
the local \term\{number-of-future-payments\} $n$ to count the included future
rows. At row $k$, write the \term\{signed-cash-flow\} as $CF_k$, its
\term\{payment-time\} as $t_k$, and the matching \term\{discount-factor\} as
$D(0,t_k)$. Write the schedule's \term\{present-value\} at
\term\{valuation-time\} $0$ as $PV_0$.

The present value of the deterministic schedule is

$$
PV_0=\sum_{k=1}^{n}CF_kD(0,t_k).
$$

The two bookkeeping symbols are local to this page; the financial quantities
come from the shared registry.

<CompactExamples summary="Worked examples (3)">
  <CompactExample label="One payment">

For a deterministic USD 1,000 receipt paid after two years with
$D(0,2)=0.9070294785$,

$$
PV_0
=1000\times0.9070294785
=\text{USD }907.0294785
\approx\text{USD }907.03.
$$

The output unit is USD at \term\{valuation-time\} from the holder's
perspective.

  </CompactExample>
  <CompactExample label="Mixed signed cash flows">

Suppose the holder receives USD 100 at $t_1$, pays USD 40 at $t_2$, and
receives USD 300 at $t_3$. The supplied discount factors are 0.97, 0.93, and
0.88.

| Payment index $k$ | Cash flow $CF_k$, USD | Discount factor $D(0,t_k)$ | Discounted cash flow $CF_kD(0,t_k)$, USD |
| ----------------: | --------------------: | -------------------------: | ---------------------------------------: |
|                 1 |                  +100 |                       0.97 |                                   +97.00 |
|                 2 |                   -40 |                       0.93 |                                   -37.20 |
|                 3 |                  +300 |                       0.88 |                                  +264.00 |

Therefore,

$$
PV_0=97.00-37.20+264.00=\text{USD }323.80.
$$

Discounting preserves the declared signs. Reversing perspective would negate
the entire result.

  </CompactExample>
  <CompactExample label="Additivity">

Concatenating two schedules retains every row, including duplicate payments.
If the two schedules use the same valuation time, perspective, currency, and
discount factors, then

$$
PV_0(\text{combined schedule})
=PV_0(\text{first schedule})+PV_0(\text{second schedule}).
$$

This identity is covered by a domain property test. The compatibility
conditions matter: values from different currencies or valuation dates cannot
be added without another model.

  </CompactExample>
</CompactExamples>

## Model boundary and review note

This is deterministic present value. It omits curves, calendars, uncertainty,
credit, recovery, liquidity, tax, and funding.

The rule $PV_0=\sum_k CF_k\,D(0,t_k)$ follows Tuckman &
Serrat\cite\{tuckman-serrat-fixed-income\}\{§1.2, price as the sum of each cash
flow times its discount factor (eqs. 1.1-1.3); full price equals present value
(eq. 1.5)\}; Hull states the same
principle\cite\{hull-options-futures\}\{Ch. 4, "Bond Pricing"\}. The lesson
stays `draft` pending human confirmation of the printed locators.
