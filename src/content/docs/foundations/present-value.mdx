## What you will be able to do

After this lesson, you should be able to:

- interpret present value from the same perspective as its cash flows;
- discount each dated deterministic amount;
- add discounted amounts without mixing payment dates.

## Make values comparable before adding

The \term\{present-value\} of a deterministic schedule is

$$
\explain{present-value}{PV_0}
=
\sum_{\explain{present-value-payment-index}{k}=1}^{
  \explain{number-of-future-payments}{n}
}
\explain{signed-cash-flow}{CF_k}
\explain{discount-factor}{D(0,\explain{payment-time}{t_k})}.
$$

The formula references definitions rather than repeating an explanation
dictionary on this page. The two page-local bookkeeping symbols are resolved
only here; the financial quantities come from the shared registry.

## Example 1: one payment

For a deterministic USD 1,000 receipt at $t=2$ with
$D(0,2)=0.9070294785$,

$$
PV_0
=1000\times0.9070294785
=\text{USD }907.0294785
\approx\text{USD }907.03.
$$

The output unit is USD at \term\{valuation-time\} from the holder's
perspective.

## Example 2: mixed signed cash flows

Suppose the holder receives USD 100 at $t_1$, pays USD 40 at $t_2$, and
receives USD 300 at $t_3$. The supplied discount factors are 0.97, 0.93, and
0.88.

| Payment index $k$ | Cash flow $CF_k$, USD | Discount factor $D(0,t_k)$ | Discounted cash flow $CF_kD(0,t_k)$, USD |
| ----------------: | --------------------: | -------------------------: | ---------------------------------------: |
|                 1 |                  +100 |                       0.97 |                                   +97.00 |
|                 2 |                   -40 |                       0.93 |                                   -37.20 |
|                 3 |                  +300 |                       0.88 |                                  +264.00 |

Therefore,

$$
PV_0=97.00-37.20+264.00=\text{USD }323.80.
$$

Discounting preserves the declared signs. Reversing perspective would negate
the entire result.

## Example 3: additivity

Let $A\mathbin{\|}B$ mean concatenating two schedules while retaining every
row, including duplicate payments. If schedules $A$ and $B$ use the same
valuation time, perspective, currency, and discount factors, then

$$
PV_0(A\mathbin{\|}B)=PV_0(A)+PV_0(B).
$$

This identity is covered by a domain property test. The compatibility
conditions matter: values from different currencies or valuation dates cannot
be added without another model.

## Model boundary and review note

This is deterministic present value. It omits curves, calendars, uncertainty,
credit, recovery, liquidity, tax, and funding. The exact textbook locator must
be verified by a human before review.
